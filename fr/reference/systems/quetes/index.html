Journal de quêtes¶

Les quêtes du jeu et leurs étapes, qui avancent toutes seules : sur un événement (une règle du Déclencheur, une réponse d'un Dialogue, un ennemi vaincu), une marque du Dialogue, des objets portés ou remis, un lieu atteint, un temps tenu. Une étape peut avoir d'autres issues qui mènent ailleurs (des branches), être facultative, ou échouer pour plusieurs motifs. Une quête finie peut donner une récompense ; une quête peut aussi échouer (un événement, une marque, un temps écoulé, un objet perdu, le joueur trop loin) ou être abandonnée depuis le journal, puis être reprise au début, à l'étape ratée ou au dernier point de reprise. Une quête peut aussi être un modèle, avec des trous comme {lieu} : pendant la partie, quetes.creer en fait une quête réelle (« Livraison 3 »), ses trous remplis par un script, une règle du Déclencheur ou un tirage (un commerce au hasard, à telle distance). Un journal s'ouvre avec J, et une notification annonce chaque nouvelle quête, chaque étape et chaque fin.

It resumes from where its setting reprise says (the start, the failed step or the last checkpoint). Nothing happens if it has not failed or cannot be taken up again (reprendre).
joueur.reapparu joueur (id), cible (id), position (vector) A dead player stands up again (death and respawn module): respawned, or brought back by sante.ranimer.
inventaire.change objet (text), quantite (number), total (number), chargement (boolean?), fiche (number?), jete (boolean?) The quantity of an item changed. total is the new quantity. chargement is true when a saved game gives the items back (game.load): nothing was picked up. For a unique copy (version 15), objet is its kind, fiche its record's number, total how many of that kind. With several stacks of a kind (version 21), total counts all of them; jete is true when a slot was dropped on the ground.
dialogue.marque marque (text) A dialogue set a mark: a word the game remembers (a line shown, an answer chosen). Every dialogue's conditions can use it.
zone.entree cible (id), qui (id), joueur (id?) Something entered a zone (an object carrying the zone system): a player, a character or a moving object, depending on what the zone detects (joueur: every player). joueur is set when qui is a player. Announced by the engine.
vehicule.monte cible (id), qui (id), joueur (id?) A player got into a vehicle (an object carrying the vehicule system) and now drives it: qui and joueur are their character (the host's, or a guest's). Announced by the engine.
vehicule.descendu cible (id), qui (id), joueur (id?) A player got out of a vehicle. Announced by the engine.
jour_nuit.lever jour (number) The sun rose: day jour begins. Lamps tagged for the night went out.
progression.niveau joueur (id?), cle (text), piste (text), niveau (number), ancien (number) Sent by the engine for each level a player reached on a track (several levels at once: several events), also when an admin sets a level. The Progression module gives the rewards of that level; a Trigger rule can play a sound or open a door.

The default applies fin_objet (the kind of item of e.objet or e.ressource), fin_par_joueur (e.source, e.joueur or e.qui is the player or the vehicle they drive) and fin_compter (e.quantite, otherwise 1). Quest log (quetes)¶

The game's quests and their steps, written in its settings and moved on by events, without code. Notifications announce each new quest, step done, failure and resume; the current steps show on the right of the screen; a log window opens with the control journal (J, or → on a gamepad). Add it once with modules_add; it is for the whole game, not placed on objects. Use it whenever the user talks about quests, missions, objectives or tasks: set its list with modules_set, never write a quest in a script, never copy a quest into a script, never edit the system (override its hooks with game.override).

The list quetes¶

Its game setting quetes is a list of rows, in order. Its kinds (sorte):

sorte What it is Under
quete Starts a quest; the rows after it belong to it. —
etape A step: texte (what to do, as the log writes it) and fin (what ends it). its quest
issue Another way to end the step right above it ("Other way to end the step"), with its own texte, end fields and suite. right under its step
test An instant switch on a mark or an item carried. its quest
echec "Also fails if": one more failure reason, for the quest (right under the quete row) or for one step (right under it). its quest or step
condition "Only if": a prerequisite of the quest; without it, the quest is not given. its quest
tirage "Random draw": fills a hole of a template (modele) when a quest is made from it. its template
recompense "Reward": one more reward, given at the end of the row above it. right under its quest, step or issue

Step numbers (in events, saves and the map's quete_etape) are the rank among the etape rows of the quest, from 1; issue, test and echec rows are not counted.

quete: titre (shown, and names the quest in events), description, depart (when the player gets it): debut (game start), evenement (the event depart_evenement: a dialogue answer's annonce, a declencheur rule), marque (the Dialogue mark depart_marque, at once if set), quete (when the quest titled depart_quete ends: a chain). Also dans_l_ordre (on: one step after the other; off: all at once), recompense and recompense_quantite (items given at the end; needs inventaire), abandon (an Abandonner button in the log, pressed twice), reprendre (on: what gives it gives it again after a failure or an abandon; a quest starting with the game is given again by quetes.demarrer), marque_echec and annonce_echec (a mark and an event when it fails or is abandoned). donneur (a scene object, optional): the character above whom the carte shows a "!" while the quest can be given; empty, every character whose dialogue sends depart_evenement or sets depart_marque gets it. Set it when a declencheur rule gives the quest near someone.

Step ends (fin), on etape and issue:

  • evenement: the event fin_evenement happens fin_fois times while the step is active. Filters: fin_cible (only about an object of that name: "Loup" counts "Loup 2"), fin_objet (only that kind of item, from the event's objet or ressource, any case: boutique.achat + "Pomme"), fin_compter (each event counts its quantite; fin_fois is the total), fin_par_joueur (only if the player caused it, its source, or the vehicle they drive; with several players, only the author's copy counts, or any player with groupe). "Defeat", "talk", "buy" are evenement steps (sante.mort, dialogue.fin, boutique.achat): never ask for another kind.
  • marque: the Dialogue mark fin_marque is set (at once if it already is).
  • objet: the player carries fin_quantite of fin_objet (from the inventory).
  • remettre: on fin_evenement (for fin_cible, such as dialogue.fin with "Meunier"), if the player carries fin_quantite of fin_objet, the inventory takes them and the step is done; otherwise an announcement says how many are missing. "Bring 3 pelts to the miller".
  • lieu: the player reaches the scene object fin_lieu: within fin_rayon metres, measured flat (it may move: a character, a car), or, if it carries the zone system, when they enter the zone. Write this rather than evenement + zone.entree. An object missing from the scene: the step waits (one line in the game log).
  • tenir: hold on fin_duree seconds of play while the step is active; with fin_lieu, time only passes within fin_rayon of it. "Survive 90 s", "Defend the farm 60 s". Time waits during a pause; the tracker and the log show what is left ("Reste 0:42").

For any other condition ("only at night"), a game script overrides quetes.compte, which says how much an event counts for a step (0: nothing): game.override("quetes.compte", function(default, e, quete, etape) if quete == "Chasse nocturne" and not shared.nuit then return 0 end return default(e, quete, etape) end) (etape is the step's row, with numero; shared.nuit stands for your game's condition).

On every step and issue: marque (a mark set when it ends, seen by every dialogue) and annonce (an event sent when it ends, for a declencheur rule: a bridge appears). Also on the quest itself (at its end): reputation (a reputation track of the progression system, such as bandits) and reputation_valeur (added to it, negative to lower it: "the bandits like the player less after this branch"); with groupe, every player present. Never a script for it. A step also has annonce_debut: an event sent when it starts (and again when it starts over or the quest resumes at it), for a declencheur rule that makes what this step needs appear (copier, montrer): the wolves of this step only, the parcel again.

Rewards¶

The quest's recompense/recompense_quantite (and xp/piste_xp) stay its first reward. For more, add recompense rows right under the quest (given when it ends), a step (when the step itself is done, optional ones too) or an issue (when that way is taken). Each row gives one thing, chosen by donne: objet (recompense and recompense_quantite, into the inventory; with groupe, as recompense_groupe says), xp (donne_xp on the Progression track donne_piste, empty: the main one; with groupe, every player present), butin (the butin table donne_table, rolled and dropped near the player) or marque (the mark donne_marque). Each is given once: never again on a load, a resume after a failure or a step done again after a branch back; only a repetable quest that starts over gives them again. The log shows the items and XP to come ("Reward: Gold x50, 100 XP"); the end announcement names them. Never a script or a declencheur rule for a quest's reward.

Recipe, a quest with three rewards: {"sorte": "quete", "titre": "The mill", "recompense": "Or", "recompense_quantite": 50}, {"sorte": "recompense", "donne": "xp", "donne_xp": 100}, {"sorte": "recompense", "donne": "butin", "donne_table": "<a table of the user's butin>"}, {"sorte": "etape", "texte": "Find the miller", "fin": "marque", "fin_marque": "meunier_vu"}, {"sorte": "recompense", "donne": "objet", "recompense": "Pain", "recompense_quantite": 2} (the bread comes when this step is done). Use only items, tables and tracks the game has.

Branches (quests with dans_l_ordre on), the same keys as the dialogue¶

  • etiquette on a step or a test names it. suite on a step or an issue says where the quest goes once it is done: a label (backwards too: the steps gone back to are to do again, counts and times from zero), fin (the quest succeeds), echec (it fails), or empty: the next row. A missing label: the quest waits there, and the game log says so (quetes: step 3 of 'X' leads to no label 'Y').
  • issue rows: the step and its other ways are active together; the first one done wins, the others close. A player's choice in a Dialogue: each answer sets a mark or sends an event, each issue waits for one. Never invent an event to jump to a label: send the event an issue waits for.
  • test rows: when the quest reaches one, it looks at once at test marque (test_marque set?) or objet (carrying test_quantite of test_objet?) and goes to si_oui or si_non (label, fin, echec, empty: the next row). For a mark set elsewhere (the player betrayed the king). Tests leading to each other stop after 100 jumps (game log).
  • With dans_l_ordre off, suite and tests are ignored (game log); issue rows still work.
  • The editor's quest map ("Show the map", editable like the dialogue's) changes these same rows: dragging an arrow sets its suite (or si_oui, si_non, alors_suite) to the target's etiquette, giving the target one from its text if it has none; an added step goes at the end of its quest with its own etiquette (the last step's empty suite becomes fin, so nothing else changes); removing a step sets what named it to fin. To link or add yourself, write the same rows: an etiquette on the target, the label in suite, a new etape row before the next quete row. Its "All quests" view draws the chains (depart: quete, depart_quete): nothing to write for it.
  • facultative on a step: optional (a bonus, a hidden chest). It becomes active and the quest moves on at once; it stays doable until the quest ends, which does not wait for it. Its marque and annonce are its reward. An echec row under it makes it missed ("find the chest within 2 minutes"), never the quest failed.

Prerequisites and repeatable quests¶

  • condition rows (any number, under the quest): condition niveau (the player's level on the Progression track condition_piste, empty: the main level, at least condition_niveau; with groupe, any player's), objet / sans_objet (carrying at least condition_quantite of condition_objet, or not), marque / sans_marque (condition_marque set, or not), quete (the quest condition_quete is at condition_etat, finie by default). The same keys as a dialogue answer's condition. While one is missing, whatever gives the quest (its event, mark, quetes.demarrer) is ignored (one line in the game log: quetes: 'X' is not available yet) and the carte shows its giver's "!" in grey (icone_indisponible_couleur); a quest given at the start, by a mark or by another quest is given as soon as everything is met. "Only from level 5" is a condition row, never a script or a mark.
  • repetable on the quest: non (default: once), toujours (what gives it gives it again as soon as it is done), chaque_jour (given again on another day than its last end; a quest given at the start, by a mark or by another quest comes back by itself that day). The day is set by the game setting jour_repetition: jeu (default: a day of the game starts at the jour_nuit sunrise, jour_nuit.lever; without that system the day never changes) or reel (the computer's date, game.today()). Saves keep the day of the last end. The log writes a repeatable quest already done "(available)" or "(again tomorrow)". A daily delivery: {"sorte": "quete", "titre": "Delivery", "depart": "evenement", "depart_evenement": "delivery.accepted", "repetable": "chaque_jour"}.
  • A character who offers it only when it can be given: a dialogue test quete with test_etat disponible.

Templates: quests made during the game¶

  • A quest with modele on is a template: it never starts by itself and is not in the log. Its texts and its object fields (fin_lieu, echec_lieu, reprise_lieu, donneur) and fin_cible have holes {name} ({cible}, {lieu}, {objet}, {n}, any name). game.emit("quetes.creer", {modele = "Delivery", valeurs = {lieu = id}, titre = "...", graine = 7}) (or the template's own depart evenement) makes a real quest from it: its rows copied, holes filled, its own title (titre, holes allowed; empty: the template's title and the next number, "Delivery 3"), then it starts. Saves keep it (same title, values, progress); quetes.creee (quete, modele, valeurs) announces it.
  • Values, first found wins: the event's valeurs; a field of the event with the hole's name (a declencheur rule envoyer sends cible, its object: {cible}); then the template's tirage rows. A tirage row (under the template): tirage_trou (the hole's name, no braces), tirage objet (a scene object carrying the tag tirage_etiquette, between tirage_distance_min and tirage_distance_max metres from the player, flat; two holes of one quest never get the same object) or nombre (a whole number from tirage_nombre_min to tirage_nombre_max). The draw uses graine (same seed, same result). In an object field a hole gets the object's id, in a text its name. {n} in a step's texte also sets its number to reach (fin_quantite, fin_fois, fin_duree).
  • A town's shops are tagged commerce and commerce:<kind> (city_get, building): they are draw candidates with nothing more. Never place objects or tag them for a template unless the user asks.
  • Nothing to draw, a hole with no value, an unknown template, unmet condition rows: nothing is created, quetes.creation_impossible (modele, motif: modele_inconnu, pas_un_modele, aucun_candidat, valeur_manquante, conditions, titre_pris; raison, also in the game log). A declencheur rule "When the event" on it can tell the player.
  • Recipe, a generated delivery (write the template; never write the deliveries themselves): {"sorte": "quete", "titre": "Delivery", "modele": true, "depart": "evenement", "depart_evenement": "delivery.ask", "description": "A parcel for {lieu}."}, {"sorte": "tirage", "tirage_trou": "depart", "tirage_etiquette": "commerce:boulangerie", "tirage_distance_max": 200}, {"sorte": "tirage", "tirage_trou": "lieu", "tirage_etiquette": "commerce:superette", "tirage_distance_min": 150, "tirage_distance_max": 600}, {"sorte": "etape", "texte": "Pick up the parcel at {depart}", "fin": "lieu", "fin_lieu": "{depart}"}, {"sorte": "etape", "texte": "Deliver it to {lieu}", "fin": "lieu", "fin_lieu": "{lieu}"}; a dialogue answer annonce: "delivery.ask" (or a declencheur rule envoyer) gives one more each time. The carte shows the drawn place's marker like any lieu step.
  • Daily quests: a template with repetable chaque_jour makes par_jour quests (default 1) per day (the day of jour_repetition: the game's sunrise or the real date), each from a new draw, so a different mission every day. When depends on its depart: debut (or a mark, or another quest finished): all of them at the start of the game and at each new day; evenement (a quest giver's dialogue answer annonce, a declencheur rule): one each time, up to par_jour, then nothing before the next day (the giver's "!" turns grey; one line in the game log). hier: garder (default: yesterday's unfinished quest stays) or retirer (abandoned on the new day, not a failure: quetes.abandon). Saves keep the last day and its count: loading on the same day makes nothing again. A quest made by it is not repeatable itself.
  • Recipe, the delivery of the day (write the template; never the deliveries): {"sorte": "quete", "titre": "Delivery of the day", "modele": true, "repetable": "chaque_jour", "par_jour": 1, "hier": "retirer", "description": "A parcel for {lieu}."}, {"sorte": "tirage", "tirage_trou": "lieu", "tirage_etiquette": "commerce:superette", "tirage_distance_min": 100, "tirage_distance_max": 500}, {"sorte": "etape", "texte": "Deliver the parcel to {lieu}", "fin": "lieu", "fin_lieu": "{lieu}"}. Given by a character instead: "depart": "evenement", "depart_evenement": "delivery.ask", "donneur": "<the character's id>" and his dialogue answer annonce: "delivery.ask".

Failures¶

The quest's own echec field is its main reason; echec rows add more, with the same keys. Reasons (echec):

  • aucun: never (the default; an echec row left on it does nothing);
  • evenement: echec_evenement happens, about echec_cible if given (sante.mort with "Marchand": the escorted merchant died); echec_par_joueur: only if the player caused it (sante.mort with "Passant");
  • marque: the mark echec_marque is set (a mark already set counts at once only with alors echoue);
  • temps: echec_delai seconds of play since the quest started, or, on a row under a step, since the step started ("Deliver within 60 s"); shown as "Reste 0:42";
  • objet: the player carries fewer than echec_quantite of echec_objet, once they carried enough: it does not fail before they took the parcel; handing it over with remettre is not losing it;
  • eloigne: the player is more than echec_rayon metres (flat) from the scene object echec_lieu, once they were close enough (escort); with groupe, when all the players are.

alors on an echec row says what happens: echoue (default: the quest fails; under an optional step, the step is missed), recommence (the step starts again from zero: counts, time, other ways, its annonce_debut again, announcement "Étape ratée : …"; under the quest: it resumes as reprise says, without failing), etiquette (the quest goes to the label alors_suite: a plan B, "spotted: flee").

Resuming and trying again¶

Only with reprendre on. reprise on the quest: debut (default: from the start), etape (at the failed step) or point (at the last step ticked point_de_reprise that it reached; none: the start). The steps before stay done; the quest's time limit starts again in full; the step's annonce_debut is sent again. Marks stay set and the world is not put back: a declencheur rule on that annonce_debut brings the enemies or the parcel back. A checkpoint step's reprise_lieu (a scene object): the player is put there (dead: when the mort module respawns them; with groupe, every player).

reessayer on the quest: non (default: what gives the quest gives it again), bouton (a "Try again" button under it in the log), auto (by itself after reessai_delai seconds of play). A script or a rule sends quetes.reessayer (quete) for the same effect.

Events, scripts, saves¶

  • Sent: quetes.debut (quete), quetes.etape_debut (quete, etape, texte), quetes.etape (quete, etape, texte; issue: rank of the other way that did it; suite: the label taken), quetes.etape_echec (quete, etape, alors; manquee for an optional step), quetes.fin (quete, facultatives {faites, total}), quetes.echec, quetes.abandon, quetes.reprise (quete, etape: where it resumes).   • Listened: game.emit("quetes.demarrer", {quete = "..."}) (without quete: the first quest not started), quetes.echouer, quetes.abandonner, quetes.reessayer (quete), quetes.creer (modele, valeurs, titre, graine: a quest from a template, above). Sent too: quetes.creee, quetes.creation_impossible.
  • Another system needing the quests in progress reads shared.quetes_en_cours[player](): {title -> {faites, actives, chemin, issues, manquees, cibles, lieux, place, suivie, rendre}} (suivie: the tracked quest; rendre: lower-case names to talk to or hand something to for an active step). Restyle them with screens_set; never redraw them with game.hud_*. B or Escape closes the log.
  • Texts follow the player's language. The log's own texts are built-in (French and English), listed by translations_get: translate them there for other languages. Titles and steps are translated with the game's tables; events keep the original title. To change a word: game.override("quetes.texte", function(default, key) if key == "titre" then return "Missions" end return default(key) end) (keys in modules_info).
  • Testing: in game_run, do what each step asks (walk to the place, wait out a time limit), then read the report's hud: Suivi des quêtes/Étape#1, Suivi des quêtes/Reste, Annonces des quêtes/Annonce#1, and, with journal pressed, Journal de quêtes/Quête#2, Journal de quêtes/Étape#3. A script that game.logs the quetes.* events shows the order (step taken, issue, failure, resume).

Several players¶

By default each player has their own quests, steps, log and rewards. For a cooperative game, tick groupe (« Quêtes du groupe »; never copy quests between players in a script): any player moves a step on, everyone sees the same state, an event that names no player counts once, a newcomer finds where the group is. recompense_groupe: groupe (once, into the Inventory's shared bag with sac_commun, else the finisher's bag) or chacun (every player present). quetes.* events are then sent once with groupe: true and joueur the player who moved it on. Pair it with sac_commun and chests in mode groupe (coffres).
