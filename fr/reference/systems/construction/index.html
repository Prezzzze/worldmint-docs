Construction¶

Le joueur pose des pièces dans le monde : murs, sols, feu de camp, n'importe quel modèle. En mode construction (B), un aperçu se pose là où il vise, sur une grille ou librement : avec la vue (le centre de l'écran) quand il joue un personnage, avec le curseur vu de haut. Un clic pose la pièce et retire son coût de l'inventaire. Une pièce posée se démolit, et rend une part de son coût.

Événements¶

Les descriptions viennent du code et sont en anglais, la langue de travail de l'agent.

Ce qu'il annonce¶

Événement Champs Description
construction.pose cible (id), piece (text) The player placed a piece (a copy of its model, cible), and its cost left the inventory. piece is its name (original language).
construction.detruit cible (id), piece (text) A placed piece is gone: torn down by the player, by construction.detruire, or killed (sante.mort). Its place on the grid is free again.
construction.mode actif (boolean) The building mode opened (actif true: the cursor shows and a preview follows it) or closed.
construction.manque piece (text), manque (text) The player tried to place a piece without its cost: manque lists what is missing, in the player's language.
joueur.occupe occupe (boolean), par (text) The player's hands are taken by something else than fighting (version 23): a building mode where the click places a piece, a tool being aimed. occupe true when it starts, false when it ends; par names who says it ("construction"), so several can overlap. Fighting modules do not strike nor block while any of them keeps the player busy.
inventaire.ajouter objet (text), quantite (number), joueur (id?) Ask the inventory to add items, such as a reward. Answered by inventaire.change, or inventaire.plein. With several players, joueur says whose bag; without it, the player whose action is being handled, or every player.
inventaire.retirer objet (text), quantite (number) Ask the inventory to remove items, such as a payment. Answered by inventaire.change, or inventaire.manque if there are not enough.

Ce qu'il écoute¶

Événement Champs Description
construction.detruire cible (id), rendre (boolean?) Ask the building system to remove a placed piece (a storm, an enemy, a script). rendre true gives back part of its cost, as tearing it down would.
inventaire.change objet (text), quantite (number), total (number), chargement (boolean?) The quantity of an item changed. total is the new quantity. chargement is true when a saved game gives the items back (game.load): nothing was picked up.
inventaire.manque objet (text), quantite (number) Items could not be removed: there are not enough.
sante.mort cible (id), source (id?) An object's health reached zero. What happens next belongs to the game: the health module does nothing else, unless its setting says to destroy the object.
langue.change langue (text) Sent by the engine when the player changes the game's language (game.set_language, the Menu options). A module redraws what it already shows: its texts go through game.t and game.language.

Building (construction)¶

The player places pieces in the world (walls, floors, a fence, a campfire: any model of the library), pays them from the inventory, and can tear them down. Add it once with modules_add (with inventaire). Use it whenever the user talks about building a shelter, a base, a camp: never write a building script.

Its game setting pieces (modules_set) is a list of rows, in order. A piece row is a piece; the cout rows under it are its cost.

  • piece: nom (shown, in the user's language; it names the piece in events), modele (the id of an object of the scene copied at each placement: create it once, standing on the ground where nothing walks, with visible: false, a collider if it should stop the player, and the systems each copy should have), decalage (on a grid, metres pushed forward along its facing: half a cell for a wall that edges the cell).
  • cout: objet (an inventory kind, as collecte gives it), nombre. A piece without cost is free.
  • Example: [{"sorte": "piece", "nom": "Mur en bois", "modele": "mur_modele", "decalage": 1}, {"sorte": "cout", "objet": "Bois", "nombre": 4}, {"sorte": "piece", "nom": "Feu de camp", "modele": "feu_modele"}, {"sorte": "cout", "objet": "Pierre", "nombre": 3}].
  • Never guess a model's size: measure it (the model's bounds in scene_get, or game.bounds in a script) to choose grille and decalage. The module itself puts each piece's foot on the surface aimed at, from the model's own bounds.
  • A piece that does something: its model's systems go to every copy. A campfire model carrying fabrication (nom_etabli « Feu de camp ») and particules becomes a workbench with flames; a bed model carrying besoins (source_besoin « Fatigue », source_heures 8) lets the player sleep.
  • Playing: the control build (B, or RB on a gamepad) opens the building mode: a preview of the piece goes where the player aims (it blinks where it cannot go, beyond portee too). build_place (click, or X) places it and takes its cost; build_rotate (wheel, or right stick pressed) turns it by rotation degrees; build_next (Q, or ←) or the keys 1 to 9 choose the piece; build_remove (X key, or B) tears down the piece aimed at and gives back rendu % of its cost. build again leaves the mode. A line at the bottom tells the piece, its cost and the keys (aide). While the mode is open the module sends joueur.occupe (occupe true, par « construction »), so combat.basique neither strikes nor blocks: the click places.
  • Aiming (game setting visee): auto (default) follows the game's camera (game.camera_view()): a character in first or third person aims with the view, the centre of the screen, and the camera keeps turning with the mouse, like the building mode of every survival game; a top-down view (the player camera's lowest tilt is 45° or more) or a placed camera aims with the cursor, which shows during the mode. centre or curseur force one. With a gamepad, the view aims (from above: the ground just in front of the player). Leave auto unless the user asks.
  • Game settings: grille (cell size in metres; 0: free placement exactly where the player aims), visee, rotation, portee (metres from the player), rendu, demolir (the player may tear down), aide.
  • Events announced: construction.pose (cible: the new piece, piece: its name), construction.detruit (cible, piece), construction.mode (actif), construction.manque (piece, manque). Send construction.detruire (cible, rendre?) to remove a piece (a storm, an enemy). Placed pieces carry the tag « construction.piece »; the preview carries « construction.apercu ».
  • Forbid building somewhere from your own script, never the module's: game.override("construction.permise", function(default, piece, position) if game.distance(position, game.find("Village")) < 30 then return false, game.t("Pas dans le village.") end return default(piece, position) end).
  • Saved games: game.save keeps the pieces (the engine keeps the copies, the module which piece each one is); game.load gives them back. In a large world, placed pieces stay loaded and asleep when far, like everything a system follows.
  • HUD keys start with construction..
  • Testing: in game_run, give the cost with inventaire.ajouter in a test script, press build, then aim: with the view (third person), turn the camera with pitch/yaw until the centre of the screen is on the ground in front of the player (the default tilt already looks about 4 m ahead); with the cursor (top-down, visee « curseur »), use mouse ([x, y] of the screen). Press build_place; read construction.pose in the report's events, then build_remove on the same spot.

Several players¶

One copy per player: each player has their own build mode, piece, preview and cost (their own inventory), aiming with their own camera (and cursor, from above). What is built is shared: every player sees it (the pieces are shared copies), and the host's saved game keeps it.
