Inventaire¶

Ce qui porte ce système se ramasse, et le joueur le garde, rangé par sorte. Le jeu peut en donner, en retirer, et réagir à chaque changement. En option, un poids : chaque objet pèse, le joueur a une charge maximale, et trop porter le ralentit.
Identifiant inventaire
Version 32
Se pose sur des objets oui
Active aussi Interaction
Demande aussi, sur le même objet Interaction

Inventory (inventaire)¶

Keeps what the player carries, grouped by kind. Activating it activates the interaction module; placing it on an object places interaction there too, so the player can use it.

  • An item to pick up: an object carrying this system (systems_attach), or every child of a group carrying it. Its kind is nom if set (systems_set), otherwise its name without a trailing number: "Apple 3" is an "Apple". quantite is how many one pickup gives: a bag of 50 coins. Name items in the user's language.
  • Picking up: the player uses the item (E by default), or walks over it if the game setting au_contact is on. The item leaves the world.
  • Give or take items from your scripts, such as a reward or a payment: game.emit("inventaire.ajouter", {objet = "Apple", quantite = 2}), game.emit("inventaire.retirer", {objet = "Gold", quantite = 10}).
  • Know what the player has: listen to inventaire.change (objet, quantite, total). A shop checks total before selling, or listens to inventaire.manque, sent when there are not enough.
  • inventaire.plein is sent when every slot holds another kind of item (game setting places).
  • The content shows at the top right with the HUD key inventaire.
  • The window: the control inventory (I, or Back on a gamepad) opens a grid of every place of the bag (places slots, empty ones included, colonnes per row), and closes it (game setting fenetre). Rows that do not fit scroll with the wheel; pause_fenetre stops the world while it is open. With disposition_fenetre grille (default) it is the system's screen « Inventaire » (version 30, modules/inventaire/ecrans/inventaire.json): Inventaire/Fenêtre, Inventaire/Titre, Inventaire/Poids, the list Inventaire/Cases whose rows are the places, Inventaire/Case#N (full or empty, the empty one fainter; same theme role case.inventaire), N being the slot's number in the grid, and Inventaire/Bouton Fermer. Change its look in the screen editor (styles, theme), never in the script. The three-part window is still drawn by the script, with keys starting with inventaire. (inventaire.case.N, inventaire.place.N). Beyond 200 places (a weight-only game with places 1000), the grid shows the rows up to the last item and one empty row more.
  • Each item keeps its slot (version 28): dragged onto an empty slot it goes there (with Shift, half of a stack starts a new stack there); dragged onto another kind (or a full stack, or a unique copy) the two slots swap; onto a stack of the same kind they merge as before. What is picked up or given goes into the first free slot; « Trier » (three-part window) packs everything at the start, in sorted order; emptying a slot leaves it empty (nothing shifts). Saved games keep each item's slot (case in the save, written only when it is not the next one, so a bag never rearranged saves exactly as before); an older save fills the first slots in order. Each player, guests included, has their own (the host keeps a guest's bag with its slots).
  • Gamepad and keyboard: the first slot has the focus, the d-pad or the arrows move it, A or Enter chooses it, B or Escape closes the window (Inventaire/Bouton Fermer is its back button; inventaire.fermer in three parts).
  • Saved games: game.save keeps what the player carries, and game.load gives it back; then the inventory announces each kind again with inventaire.change and chargement = true (nothing was picked up), so that every system knows the totals. Do not re-add items after a load.
  • The item in hand (game setting barre, on by default): a bar of cases_barre slots (5) at the bottom of the screen (place_barre; afficher_barre off if the game draws its own). It is the system's screen « Barre rapide » (version 30): Barre rapide/Fond, the lists Barre rapide/Rangée (keys 1 to 9...) and Barre rapide/Seconde rangée (Shift), each slot Barre rapide/Case#N with Icône#N (the kind's image) and Texte#N (Case 2#N, Icône 2#N, Texte 2#N in the second row); the script places it and lays its rows out for place_barre. What is picked up fills the first free slot; keys 1 to 9 (then 0, - and =, up to 12 slots; a second row with rangees_barre: see version 27 below), the wheel (item_next, item_previous) and the d-pad left (item_next: it goes round the slots; every other gamepad button already belongs to a system) choose the slot, and its key again puts the item away. What is held is announced by inventaire.en_main (objet, "" for empty hands, avant, place): collecte gathers with the tool held, a game script equips a sword with it. Choose it from a script with game.emit("inventaire.tenir", {objet = "Axe"}) (or place, or objet = ""); objet and place together put that item at that slot of the bar and hold it (what the slot held takes the slot it left): a game window that drags an item onto the bar sends just that, without taking the item out of the bag. In the window, a number key puts the hovered slot at that place of the bar. The bar, the chosen slot and what is held are saved; each player has their own.
  • Its model in the hand (montrer_en_main, on): a copy of its model follows the character's hand bone, found by measuring the skeleton (game.hand_bone: a bone made for holding under the right hand, else the right hand; os_main overrides it). The model comes from the list en_main (a row per kind: objet, modele, an object of the scene, usually hidden, and an offset and rotation along the bone), else from the first item of that kind picked up. An item the game gives (at the start, a reward, crafted) was never picked up: without its en_main row, nothing shows in the hand (the game log says inventaire: « Hache » est tenu sans modèle). Give every tool and weapon its row: modele_fichier (the model file) and prise arme are enough. Best: set the model in the character's hand in the editor (entities_create with attach_bone), check it with a capture, hide it, and name it in en_main: its exact place is reused. Do not leave a visible model in the hand, nor write a script that shows or hides one.
  • Holding a melee weapon right (prise placee): the copy takes the exact place of its model RELATIVE TO THE HAND BONE. An example the creator put in (or right next to) the character's hand in the editor is enough as modele, even if it is not attached to a bone: its place relative to the measured hand bone, in the scene (the rest pose the editor shows), is copied. Too far from the hand (more than 15 % of the character's height), nothing is copied and the game log says attach: ... from the hand: read it after game_run. To make the example follow the hand in the editor too, attach it without moving it: entities_update with parent (the character), then a second entities_update with attach_bone (from game.hand_bone or asset_info bones) and keep_place: true (the editor's button "Attach to the hand (keep its place)" does the same); never retype its numbers.
  • Never delete, move or "clean up" an example object the creator placed (a weapon put in the hand to show the grip, even if it looks like it floats or counts as a spawn point): it is the reference. Hide it, or ask; do not remove it. Then hide it and name it in modele. Without an example, use prise arme (the grip measured on the model). Every pack differs (pivot at the pommel or the guard, long axis x, y or z, centimetres): measure or copy, never guess an axis.
  • Check every melee weapon on a capture in game, standing and during a hit (the clip of the combat module): the fist closes on the handle, no finger nor hand goes through the handle or the blade, the blade is not inside the arm or the body. One weapon held right does not mean the others are: check each row of en_main.
  • A click on a slot sends inventaire.choisi (objet, total). What it does belongs to the game: eat an apple (inventaire.retirer, then sante.soigner), equip a sword, read a letter. Write that in a game script, or a declencheur rule on the event.

A bigger bar, and two rows (version 27)¶

  • cases_barre goes from 1 to 12: keys 1 to 9, then 0, - and = for slots 10, 11 and 12 (the top row of the keyboard, as in World of Warcraft). Keys are read by their place on the keyboard, not the character they type: on an AZERTY keyboard, the two keys right of 0 () and =) are the - and = of the bar.
  • rangees_barre = 1 (default) or 2. With 2, a second row of cases_barre slots shows above the first one at the bottom (bas), below it at the top (haut), and beside it on the screen side in a column (gauche, droite). The control item_second_row (« Deuxième rangée de la barre », left Shift by default, changeable in the Options) held with a slot's key chooses the slot of the second row: Shift+2 is its second slot (it shows « Maj+2 »). The second row's slots are numbered 13 to 24 whatever cases_barre is (place of inventaire.en_main and inventaire.tenir): 13 is its first slot.
  • What is picked up fills the first row first, then the second. The wheel and the d-pad go round every slot of both rows. The three-part window shows both rows under the bag. Saved games keep both rows; each player, guests included, has their own.
  • Too many slots for the screen (12 slots on a 4:3 screen, a long column): every slot shrinks the same way, nothing goes past the screen's edge. The Progression's XP bar sits above the whole bar, both rows.
  • Fit the bar to the game. cases_barre = the number of things the player really holds: 2 for an axe and a pickaxe, 3 to 5 in a shooter, 6 to 8 in a survival game, 10 to 12 and rangees_barre 2 for an action RPG or an online RPG. Untick barre when the player never holds anything (a puzzle game, a racing game).

Kinds of items and unique copies (version 15, off by default)¶

The list sortes_objets (« Sortes d'objets ») gives a kind more than a name: a row per kind with objet (the kind), description (shown in the item's tooltip, translated like the game's texts), categorie (free: arme, armure, outil, gemme... the coming Equipment and Loot modules read it), rarete (the default rarity written on a new copy's record), icone (« Image », version 25: see below) and uniques (« Exemplaires uniques », off). A kind missing from the list, or with uniques off, stacks exactly as before, saved games included.

The item's picture (version 25): the column icone (an image file; an image given to modules_set is copied into the project, like every image setting). Every slot that shows the item then shows its picture filling the slot (proportions kept, a small margin) with the quantity small in a corner (« x4 »), the name staying in the tooltip and the card: the bag (grille and trois_parties), the hotbar at the bottom of the screen and in the window, the worn equipment slots, the card (a bigger picture at its top), the searched chest or body, the Loot window, Crafting (liste and detaillee), shops (a small picture left of the name) and the Equipment window. Empty, or a file that cannot be found: the name is written as before, without any error. Give a picture to every kind of item: the game's own item pictures, or the generic icons of a UI pack (icon_*) only when one really matches the item. Other systems and a game's own windows read it with shared.inventaire_icone(kind) (the file, or nil), or game.module_settings("inventaire").sortes_objets. The hud report of game_run gives a slot button's image. In a game with several players, the picture is a game setting: each guest sees it in their own slots (one image per slot).

  • Unique copies (uniques on): each copy picked up or given gets its own record (game.item_create, kept with the world's save, written by the host only): its number, kind, rarity, displayed name, bonuses, wear, upgrade level, gem slots and gems, item level and free data (see the script reference, game.item_*). Each copy takes a slot of its own and never stacks; it weighs like one item of its kind.
  • What the inventory says: inventaire.change, inventaire.en_main, inventaire.choisi and inventaire.plein give objet = the kind and, for a unique copy, fiche = its record's number (total counts the copies of that kind). Read a copy with game.item_get(e.fiche).
  • Give a copy already made (a reward with chosen bonuses): local n = game.item_create("Épée de fer", {bonus = {{valeur = "degats", add = 4}}}), then game.emit("inventaire.ajouter", {objet = "Épée de fer", fiche = n}). Without fiche, the inventory makes a new record with the kind's default rarity.
  • Take one: game.emit("inventaire.retirer", {objet = "Épée de fer", fiche = n}) (without fiche: the first copies of that kind); their records are destroyed. Drop one: game.emit("inventaire.jeter", {objet = "Épée de fer", fiche = n}) puts it in front of the player with its record (its porteur becomes objet:<scene>/<id>); picked up again, the same record comes back.
  • The tooltip: hovering a slot of the window (or giving it the focus) shows the record's name, the kind's description and the record's bonuses (HUD key inventaire.infobulle, theme role infobulle.objet). The hook inventaire.infobulle receives {objet, fiche, joueur, bonus = {debut, fin}}: bonus says which lines are the record's bonuses, so Equipment replaces them by their total or greys them instead of adding lines. A stack without description shows no tooltip. Rarity colours come with the Loot module.
  • Several players: the records stay on the host; each guest receives the records of their own items and of those on the ground (protocol 21), so their tooltips show the same thing.

Weight (off by default)¶

In the editor, every weight setting is in the folding group « Poids » of the Inventory's card.

Ticking the game setting poids_actif (modules_set) gives every item a weight and the player a maximum load; off, nothing weighs and nothing changes. Slots (places, the value inventaire_places) and weight work together or apart: a game uses slots, weight, or both.

  • The weight of one item, in the game's unit (unite, « kg » by default, translated like the game's texts): the poids set on the item picked up (systems_set; -1: its kind's), else its kind's row in poids_objets (objet, poids: use it for what scripts give, such as gold at 0.01), else poids_defaut (1). A script can compute it with the hook inventaire.poids: game.override("inventaire.poids", function(default, objet) if objet == "Water skin" then return 0.2 + 0.8 * full end return default(objet) end).
  • The maximum load is the value charge_max (game.stat(player, "charge_max")), whose base is charge_base (50). It grows like any value: a Progression characteristic (« Force: +2 kg of load per point » is the row effet, valeur charge_max, ajout 2), a level reward (bonus on charge_max), later equipment (game.stat_bonus(player, "charge_max", "sac_a_dos", {add = 20})).
  • Overload steps (paliers_poids): each row starts beyond seuil % of the load; the highest reached applies. Its effects: ralentir (% slower: a negative bonus from the source « poids » on vitesse_deplacement, which the engine applies on the host and in a guest's prediction), sans_course, sans_saut, sans_ramasser, immobile (no step at all), endurance (% more stamina spent running, jumping and striking, with the endurance system: the value endurance_cout), message (shown on entering it; empty: « Trop chargé : 12 / 10 kg. », or « Bien trop chargé » when immobile). Leaving every step says « Vous n'êtes plus surchargé. ». Defaults: beyond 100 %, 30 % slower and no running; beyond 150 %, no step and no pickup.
  • Picking up: trop_lourd = permettre (default: picked up, the steps apply) or refuser (an item that would go beyond limite_ramasser % of the load stays on the ground, with « Enclume est trop lourd (7 kg). »). A step with sans_ramasser refuses every pickup. Both send inventaire.trop_lourd (objet, poids, porte, charge, raison limite or palier). The refused item's slot lights up for signal_refus seconds (1.5 by default, 0: never): its kind's slot in the hotbar and the window, otherwise the hotbar slot it would have gone to; the color, or the alerte image, comes from the interface theme. While the window is open, the weight message sits under it. What scripts give (inventaire.ajouter: a reward) always goes in: the steps then apply.
  • On screen: the window shows « Poids : 7 / 10 kg » under its title (Inventaire/Poids; inventaire.poids.total in three parts) and each slot the weight of its stack; a small indicator (inventaire.poids, at poids_x, poids_y; afficher_poids) says « Chargé : 8 / 10 kg » in amber (couleur_charge) from alerte_charge % (80), then each step's own libelle and couleur (by default « Surchargé : 12 / 10 kg » in orange, then « Écrasé : 16 / 10 kg » in red; the window's « Poids » line takes the step's color too); the step's message is inventaire.poids.message, on the discreet backing of announcements (inventaire.poids.message.fond, role panneau.annonce.poids), for 3 seconds, even while the window pauses the game. Restyle them by those keys like the rest of the HUD.
  • Events: inventaire.poids (poids, charge, palier the row number, 0 for none, seuil, avant) when the weight, the load or the step changes. A Trigger rule or a script reacts to it (a panting sound, a tip).
  • Several players: computed on the host, one copy per player: each guest has their own weight, load and steps, sees their own indicator and window, and their prediction walks at the slowed speed and without running, as the host makes them walk. With sac_commun, poids_sac_commun says who carries the shared bag: partage (default: its weight split equally between the players present), chacun (each carries all of it) or aucun.
  • Running and jumping are also taken by endurance (exhausted) and besoins (an empty need): each system asks again every step while it needs it, so none gives them back to a player another one still holds.

Rarity in the tooltip and « Jeter » (version 16)¶

  • The tooltip of a unique copy shows its name in its rarity's color and the rarity written under it, when the butin module sets that rarity (raretes); without it, the theme's color.
  • bouton_jeter (on): « Jeter » in the small window of a clicked slot drops it, and the control item_drop (G) drops the one hovered, with inventaire.jeter. Since version 21, stacks are dropped too (see below).

Stacks, split, merge and drop (version 21, ADR-042)¶

  • Maximum stack: the column pile_max (« Pile max. ») of sortes_objets, an integer; 0 or missing: no limit, one slot per kind, exactly as before (saved games included). With it, a kind can take several slots: the first stack keeps the kind as its key ("Bois"), the next ones are "Bois~2", "Bois~3"... A unique copy keeps "kind#record".
  • Adding fills the kind's stacks that are not full (in slot order), then opens new slots. A pickup from the world takes what fits and the object keeps the rest (inventaire.plein with reste); what scripts give (inventaire.ajouter: Loot, Crafting, a shop) is all or nothing, as before.
  • Totals never change meaning: inventaire.change.total, what Crafting, Gathering, Needs and Quests count, inventaire.retirer and inventaire.sortir all work on the kind, all its slots together. Removing takes from the smallest stack first; an empty stack frees its slot and leaves the bar. The bar holds a slot (a precise stack).
  • Events (from any script or a game's own window):
  • inventaire.jeter {objet, case?, fiche?}: drops the slot case (its key), otherwise the unique copy fiche, otherwise the first slot of kind objet. The whole slot falls in front of the player as ONE object (the kind's model in hand, the one kept at pickup, or an object of that kind in the world; with none, a builtin:cube measured then scaled to taille_jete metres), set on the measured ground, never on a body. Its prompt says « Bois x40 »; E picks the same quantity back in one go. A unique copy keeps its record. The dropped object is kept by the saved game.
  • inventaire.diviser {case, quantite?, vers?}: quantite items of that slot (missing: half, rounded down) go to a new slot right after it, or with vers onto that stack of the same kind, up to pile_max (the rest stays in case). Refused when a new slot is needed and none is free (« Plus de place dans le sac. », the slot lights up like other refusals); a stack of 1 or a unique copy does not split.
  • inventaire.jeter {case, quantite}: only quantite items of a stack fall (one object « Bois x5 »); the slot keeps the rest.
  • inventaire.regrouper {de, vers}: two stacks of the same kind merge, up to pile_max; the rest stays where it was.
  • inventaire.decrire {objet, description} (version 29): describes a kind during the game when its row of sortes_objets gives no description (that row wins); the tooltip and the three-part details show it. An empty description removes it.
  • Reading the slots one by one: shared.inventaire_contenu[player]() returns the list of slots in order, each {cle, objet, quantite, fiche} (cle is the slot key to send to the events above), and whether the bag is shared.
  • The module's window: a click on a slot (A on a gamepad) opens its small window next to it (HUD key inventaire.menu, theme role panneau.menu.inventaire): the name and quantity, « Jeter », « Diviser en deux » and a quantity (« − », « 5 sur 10 », « + », « Valider »); a unique copy or a stack of 1 only has « Jeter ». It closes with Escape or B (its « Fermer » button), a click elsewhere, or once the action is done; near the right edge it opens on the slot's left. The single click still sends inventaire.choisi (eating an apple by a click keeps working); the double click keeps its role (use, equip) and closes the small window. Right click splits a slot in two in place. Dragging a stack onto another of the same kind merges them; dragging a slot out of every window drops it. Shift + drag (the control item_split, « Diviser en glissant (tenir) », left Shift by default, read when the drag starts or when it is released) moves only half: onto an empty place of the window, a new stack; onto a stack of the same kind, up to pile_max, the rest goes back; out of the window, half falls on the ground. A stack of 1 or a unique copy drags as usual. item_split shares left Shift with run: both are held together, which is harmless (the window pauses the world, or the player is not moving). While the window pauses the game, item_drop and item_split still reach it. inventaire.choisi and inventaire.glisse also give case.
  • A game's own window (a survival game's bag): draw each slot from shared.inventaire_contenu[game.player()](), keep its cle, and add your buttons: Drop sends game.emit("inventaire.jeter", {objet = c.objet, case = c.cle}), Split sends game.emit("inventaire.diviser", {case = c.cle}) (or with quantite), dropping a stack on another sends game.emit("inventaire.regrouper", {de = a.cle, vers = b.cle}). For Shift + drag, read game.action("item_split") when the drag starts or ends and send inventaire.diviser {case, quantite = half, vers = the target stack} (or without vers onto an empty place), or inventaire.jeter {case, quantite = half} out of the window. Redraw on inventaire.change. Never edit the module.
  • Several players: each player drops and splits in their own bag; what is dropped is a world object everyone sees and can pick up.

Several players¶

One copy per player: each player has their own bag by default. The game setting sac_commun ("One bag for the whole group") gives everyone the same bag; with the weight on, poids_sac_commun says who carries it (split by default). The host's saved game keeps each guest's bag under their player key: they find it again when they come back.

For the Equipment module (version 16)¶

  • inventaire.sortir {objet, fiche?}: takes one item out of the bag without destroying it (a unique copy keeps its record).
  • A double click on a slot (or A twice on a gamepad) sends inventaire.choisi with double = true; dragging a slot onto another element of the HUD sends inventaire.glisse {objet, fiche, cible, joueur}; opening or closing the window sends inventaire.fenetre {ouverte, joueur}.
  • The hook inventaire.infobulle (lines, {objet, fiche, joueur}) -> lines: other systems add lines to an item's tooltip ([b] and [color=#rrggbb] allowed). Bonuses show their value's displayed name (game.stat_name).
  • inventaire.tenir {objet = kind} also finds a unique copy of that kind (or fiche for a precise one). With place too (version 20), that copy goes to that slot.

Version 17¶

  • The hook inventaire.porteur (default, player) -> the object that holds the item in hand (the model is attached to its hand bone). The default is the object the hook equipement.corps gives when the game overrides it (one hook for a game whose visible body is another object following the player: what is worn and the weapon in hand follow it), otherwise the player's character.
  • inventaire.tenir_porte {objet, fiche?, prioritaire?}: the Equipment module hands the weapon worn in its slot to the inventory, which holds, shows and announces it like the bar's (prioritaire: before the bar; otherwise when the bar holds nothing; objet "": none).
  • The list en_main gains modele_fichier (a model file, such as a weapon shipped in armes/), prise (placee as before; arme or debout: the measured weapon hold of ADR-034, the grip found on the model), longueur (its real length for a 1.8 m character) and avant. Online, guests load the same modele_fichier from their copy of the game and see it held the same way (protocol 23).

Version 22¶

  • prise arme is the measured hold of a melee weapon or a tool (game.attach melee): the handle is the longest round, narrow part of the model, measured slice by slice along its longest side; the fist grips it from its end away from the head (the middle of a sword grip, low on an axe haft), the head or blade forward, on the thumb side. avant forces the side of the head. A model whose handle is flat, or whose round blade is longer than its grip, is measured wrong: use placee with an example in the hand. A firearm stays held by the Shooting module (tir).
  • os_main also applies to a measured hold. A bone the character does not have (hand_r on a KayKit, which has handslot.r): the bone found by measuring the skeleton holds the item, and the game log says it once (attach: ... has no bone named ...).

The window in three parts (version 24, disposition_fenetre)¶

  • disposition_fenetre = grille (default, the window above, unchanged) or trois_parties: one window that fills the screen, as in Rust (Tab), Valheim or Enshrouded. Choose trois_parties for a survival game or an RPG (equipment, chests, bodies to search); keep grille for a small game with a few items.
  • Left (only with the equipement system): the slots the game defined in Equipment, as slots (not a list), in two columns on each side of an empty space for the character (espace_personnage, a share of the screen width, 0.14; 0 brings them together), the weapon slots at the bottom of each column (version 26: the slots spread over the whole height of the column, and the space goes from the top of the first slot to the bottom of the last one, with no slot in it). Dragging an item of the bag onto a slot wears it (equipement.porter), a click on a worn slot takes it off (equipement.retirer): the Equipment module does the rest, as always. Its own window (O) still works; it no longer opens beside the inventory (avec_inventaire) in this layout.
  • Centre: the bag (title with the slots used out of places, the weight if on), the grid (colonnes), the hotbar under it, separate (drag a bag slot onto it, drag it back onto the bag to empty that place; two bar slots swap), « Trier » (sorts by the categorie of sortes_objets, then kind, then name) and « Fermer ».
  • Right: what is searched when the player opens a chest or a body (everything that sends butin.ouvrir; the Loot module's own window then stays closed), with « Tout prendre » (role bouton.principal): dragging an item to the bag takes it (butin.prendre), dragging a bag slot onto it puts it in a chest (butin.ranger, chests only, with the chest's sharing rules). Otherwise the details of the chosen item: its name in its rarity's colour, description, bonuses (the inventaire.infobulle hook, so Equipment's comparison too), quantity, weight, and « Équiper » (bouton.principal, if equipable), « Diviser en deux », « Jeter » (bouton.danger). In this layout, a click on a slot shows its details instead of the small window, and the hover tooltip is not shown.
  • HUD keys: inventaire.fenetre, inventaire.gauche, inventaire.centre, inventaire.droite, inventaire.grille, inventaire.case.N, inventaire.fbarre.N, inventaire.equip.<slot>, inventaire.fouille.N, inventaire.fouille.tout, inventaire.trier, inventaire.equiper. Roles: fenetre.inventaire, titre.inventaire, case.inventaire, case.barre_rapide, case.equipement, case.rare, case.butin, fenetre.butin (the right column while searching), bouton.principal, bouton.danger.
  • Everything else stays: drop and split (Shift + drag), weight, stacks, unique copies, keys 1 to 9 (and 0, - and =; with two rows, inventaire.fbarre.13 to .24 under the first), gamepad (focus, A, B on « Fermer »), several players (each their own window; a guest's take or put goes through the host).
  • Behind the window, a dark veil over the whole screen (inventaire.voile, role fond.voile); dropping a dragged slot on it drops the item on the ground. A slot holding a rare-or-better copy (bag, hotbar of the window, searched content, worn slot) gets a thin frame in its rarity's colour (the one set in Loot's raretes), the panel <slot key>.cadre with role fond.case.rare: an interface pack with a case.rare row replaces it with its own image.
  • The character in the empty space (version 26, montrer_personnage, on): while the three-part window is open, the game camera turns toward the player's character, who stands in the empty space between the equipment slots, as in Baldur's Gate 3 or Diablo. The camera is made for it (game.spawn_camera), faces the character at the height of its centre, and its distance comes from the character's measured size (game.bounds) and the place of inventaire.personnage on screen: a 1 m or a 2.5 m character fills the space the same way. The character fills about 85 % of the space's height (of at most three times its width, on a narrow 4:3 screen, so that its shoulders do not go behind the slots) (framed on its height: a rest pose that is a T or an A pose makes its bounds wide, so its arms may go a little past the space rather than the character shrinking). On opening, the character is put at once in the rest state of its animation graph (its entry state, game.rest_pose), so it is never frozen mid-step, and its idle clip keeps playing while the window pauses the world. It glides there in 0.25 s and comes back to the player's camera (or to the placed camera that was active before) when the window closes, on death, on fenetres.fermer and on a scene change; the camera made is destroyed. The dark veil goes round that space, which stays clear; the window then has no background of a single block: each column has its own, and the left column's is drawn in pieces round the space (inventaire.fond.haut, .bas, .gauche, .droite). With an interface pack, they keep the window's look: their roles (fenetre.inventaire.equipement, fenetre.inventaire.sac, fenetre.inventaire.fiche) take the theme's fenetre.inventaire row. Online, each player sees their own character through their own camera, at home; nobody else sees a camera nor an object. Off, or with grille, or without Equipment: nothing changes. A wall right in front of the character can hide it: nothing moves the camera around it. Never write your own camera script for this: tick or untick the setting.
