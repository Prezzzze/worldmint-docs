Ennemis¶

Ce qui porte ce système patrouille, poursuit le joueur quand il le voit, et le frappe à portée, ou lui tire dessus de loin (balle, flèche, boule de feu), avec ses propres valeurs : un squelette du début et un boss n'ont ni la même vitesse ni les mêmes dégâts. Ils contournent les murs.

Événements¶

Les descriptions viennent du code et sont en anglais, la langue de travail de l'agent.

Ce qu'il annonce¶

Événement Champs Description
ennemi.etat cible (id), etat (text) An enemy changed state: patrouille (patrols), poursuite (chases the player), visee (a shooter aims before its first shot: the moment for a warning sound), attaque (hits in reach, or shoots), attente (waits its turn near the player, when enough others are already attacking), retour (goes back to its spot), mort (dead, does nothing more).
ennemi.tir source (id), cible (id), sorte (text) An enemy fired a shot at a player (its setting tir): sorte is balle, fleche or boule_de_feu. Announced when the shot leaves, hit or miss; a hit is then announced by combat.coup. Listen to it for a sound of your own, a shake of the screen, or to count shots.
combat.coup cible (id), source (id), montant (number) An attack hit something. The health module, if present, has been asked to remove the damage.
sante.blesser cible (id), montant (number), source (id?) Ask the health module to remove health from an object.

Ce qu'il écoute¶

Événement Champs Description
scene.change depuis (text), vers (text) Sent by the engine once the player has arrived in another scene of the game (game.go_to, or a passage). Scripts keep running across scenes: this is when a module looks at the objects of the new scene.
sante.mort cible (id), source (id?) An object's health reached zero. What happens next belongs to the game: the health module does nothing else, unless its setting says to destroy the object.
sante.change cible (id), vie (number), max (number) An object's health changed, or is announced once when the game starts.
projectile.touche projectile (number), owner (id?), cible (id?), point (vector), normale (vector), partie (text?), os (text?), vitesse (vector), eau (boolean?), data (table?) A projectile launched by game.projectile hit something: cible is the object hit (nil for the terrain and for water), partie the part of a character's body (tete, torse, bras, jambes; missing for an object), os the bone, vitesse its velocity on arrival (m/s), data the table given at launch. eau = true when it entered water (point on the surface): it goes on, strongly slowed, and sinks (put a splash there, game.effect("eclaboussure", e.point), or stop it with game.stop_projectile). Enemies (ennemis)¶

Objects carrying this system become characters with a state machine: they patrol around their spot, chase the player once they see them, hit them in reach, and go back home when they lose them. Placing it on an object places Health there too. Add it only if the user asked for enemies that attack.

  • Place it on each kind of enemy, best on a group: "Early skeletons" with low damage and speed, "Late skeletons" with high values, the boss alone with its own. Set them with systems_set, and the Health values (health, armor) the same way. A child can change one value for itself.
  • Several enemies at once: set the game setting attaquants_max (modules_set), usually 1 or 2. Only that many strike at the same time; the others wait their turn at distance_attente, circling the player, and step in when a place is free. With the default 0, all strike together: five skeletons in reach kill the player in seconds.
  • States: patrouille, poursuite, visee (a shooter aims before its first shot), attaque, attente (waits its turn), retour, mort. Each change is announced by ennemi.etat (cible, etat): listen to it for a growl, an alert icon, a music change.
  • They walk around obstacles (objects with a collider) to reach the player or go back home. A closed room keeps them out. With vue on, they still have to see the player first.
  • With vue on, walls with a collider hide the player; they give up after recherche seconds without seeing them (3 by default), or beyond abandon.
  • Their hits go through the health module, like the player's: the player's armor applies. They are announced by combat.coup. When the player dies, they all go back home.
  • Death: a dead enemy never moves nor strikes again. animation_mort (empty by default) is a clip of its own model, picked from its clips (animation_clips), never guessed: it plays once and the body stays on its last frame, seen the same by guests. Empty: it just stops where it stands; say so to the user and offer to pick a clip. corps: reste (default; Loot can still open its window on it), apres (stays duree_corps seconds, then goes), aussitot; it goes with effet_corps (retrecir, effondrer, eclater, tomber, or aucun) in duree_effet_corps seconds. Health's « remove at death » still removes it at once.
  • Animations: give the clip names of their model (read them with animation_clips); without them, nothing plays. Never guess a clip name.
  • Enemies cloned during the game from one carrying the system join in within half a second, with its values.

Enemies that shoot¶

Only if the user asked for enemies that shoot, throw or cast (soldiers, archers, a mage). Set tir on that group: balle (an instant shot, a firearm), fleche (flies, drops, sticks) or boule_de_feu (slow, straight, explodes). The other values appear once tir is set; everything is per object or group, nothing is in a script.

  • What the player sees: before its first shot the enemy aims for temps_visee seconds: it turns, leans its upper body toward the player (game.aim_at), a small glint shines (eclat_visee), and son_visee / animation_visee play if given. It aims again each time it lost sight. Never set temps_visee to 0 for ordinary enemies: the player needs that moment to take cover.
  • Walls: it never shoots through a collider. Hidden, it walks around the wall to find the player again (state poursuite); give shooters a longer recherche (6 to 10 s) than melee guards, or they give up quickly.
  • Distance: it shoots within portee_tir and keeps distance_gardee (steps back when the player comes closer, moves in when they back off, shooting as it walks; 0: it stays put). With frappe_de_pres (default), right next to the player it strikes with its melee values (portee, degats, recharge) instead.
  • Accuracy: imprecision degrees at first, going down to imprecision_min after temps_ajustement seconds of steady aim, plus imprecision_mouvement degrees per m/s of the player's speed. So: far shots often miss, a player who stays in the open gets hit more and more, running helps.
  • Rhythm: bursts of rafale shots at cadence shots per second, then pause_rafales; chargeur shots before a reload of temps_rechargement s (0: never reloads). degats_tir per shot, times multiplicateur_tete on the head (the part is measured on the player's skeleton).
  • Several at once: attaquants_max counts shooters too: aiming or shooting takes a place; the others keep their distance and wait. With 3 or more shooters, set it to 1 or 2.
  • Allies: by default its shots pass through other enemies and never hurt them (blesse_allies off). On, an enemy in the way blocks the shot, so it moves to clear its line.
  • Projectiles (fleche, boule_de_feu): vitesse_projectile (an arrow 30-50 m/s, a fireball 10-15: dodgeable), gravite_projectile (0 for a fireball, 0.3-1 for an arrow; it aims higher to make up for it), anticipation (0: aims where the player is, 1: where they will be). modele_projectile: a hidden object of the scene (an arrow, a ball), copied for each shot; without it, a bright trail shows the projectile.
  • Effects: effets_tir (default on) shows the muzzle flash, the trail, impacts and bullet holes on scenery, a puff on a body, a small explosion for a fireball. Sounds (son_tir, son_rechargement) and animations (animation_tir, animation_rechargement) are optional: clip names read on the model with animation_clips (a pistol pack has clips such as Pistol_Idle_Loop, Pistol_Shoot, Pistol_Reload: use what the model really has; for the idle clip while armed, set animation_repos to its armed idle).
  • Shipped sounds (cite "sons/<pack>/<file>", copied into the project with their licence; several separated by commas: one at random at each shot): son_tir of a gunman the family of its weapon (sons/armes-a-feu/pistolet_1.wav, sons/armes-a-feu/pistolet_2.wav, fusil_assaut_, mitraillette_, fusil_pompe_, fusil_precision_, revolver_), or its _loin file for an enemy that shoots from afar (a sniper); son_rechargement sons/rechargements/chargeur_fusil.wav (or chargeur_pistolet, pompe_armer). An archer (tir fleche): son_tir sons/arc-arbalete/arc_tir_1.wav, sons/arc-arbalete/arc_tir_2.wav (a crossbowman arbalete_tir_1..3), son_visee sons/arc-arbalete/arc_bander_1.wav. A mage (tir boule_de_feu): son_tir sons/explosions-magie/boule_de_feu_lancer_1.wav, sons/explosions-magie/boule_de_feu_lancer_2.wav. The impact of an enemy's projectile has no sound setting yet.
  • Each shot announces ennemi.tir (source, cible, sorte), hit or miss; a hit is announced by combat.coup and goes through sante.blesser (the player's armor and invulnerability apply).
