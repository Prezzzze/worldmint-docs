Population¶

Des passants qui naissent sur les trottoirs autour du joueur, hors de sa vue, marchent le long des rues et partent derrière lui. Rien n'est écrit dans la scène : seulement ces réglages. Les passants sont des copies de vos personnages modèles (cachés), réutilisées sans cesse. Ceux qui comptent ne sont jamais repris : ce que nomme une quête jusqu'à sa fin, un compagnon, un passant que le joueur a touché. Ils se souviennent : un passant parti revient le même pendant une minute, une voiture abîmée reste abîmée, et ce que le joueur laisse l'attend. Un coup de feu ou une explosion vide la rue autour : les passants fuient, les voitures qui y vont s'arrêtent.

Événements¶

Les descriptions viennent du code et sont en anglais, la langue de travail de l'agent.

Ce qu'il annonce¶

Événement Champs Description
population.apparait cible (id), sorte (text) A pedestrian appeared around the player, out of sight (Population module): a copy from the pool, tagged population.
population.disparait cible (id), sorte (text), important (boolean?) A pedestrian left (Population module): too far or behind the player, back to the pool. Its id may come back as another pedestrian. important: it had been kept (touched, named by a quest) and nothing names it any more.
population.touche cible (id), joueur (id), comment (text), sorte (text) A pedestrian became important (Population module): a player touched it (comment frappe, tue, parle, monte) or something keeps it (quetes, accompagner, garder, emmene). The director never takes it back while something names it; it stays itself.
population.fuite lieu (vector), rayon (number), passants (number) A danger made pedestrians flee (Population module): lieu is where it happened, rayon how far it scared, passants how many fled.

Ce qu'il écoute¶

Événement Champs Description
population.garder cible (id) Ask the Population module to never take back this object (it puts the tag population.garder on it).
population.liberer cible (id) Ask the Population module to let this object go again (it removes the tag population.garder).
quetes.debut quete (text), groupe (boolean?) A quest started: quete is its title. groupe (Journal de quêtes 8): true for a quest of the whole group (game setting groupe), sent once for the group, joueur being who moved it on.
quetes.creee modele (text), quete (text), valeurs (table), groupe (boolean?) A quest was made from a template (Journal de quêtes 15, events version 34): quete is its own title, modele the template's, valeurs the value of each hole (an object's id, a number, a text). Sent before its quetes.debut. groupe: true for a quest of the whole group.
quetes.etape quete (text), etape (number), texte (text), issue (number?), suite (text?), groupe (boolean?) A step of a quest is done: etape is its rank in the quest (from 1), texte what it said. Sent before quetes.fin when it was the last one. issue (Journal de quêtes 11): the rank (from 1) of the other way to end the step that did it, absent when the step itself did; texte is then that way's text. suite (Journal de quêtes 11): the label the quest goes to next (fin, echec or a step's label), absent when it simply goes on to the next row. groupe (Journal de quêtes 8): true for a quest of the whole group (game setting groupe), sent once for the group, joueur being who moved it on.
quetes.etape_debut quete (text), etape (number), texte (text), groupe (boolean?) A step of a quest starts (Journal de quêtes 11, events version 33): it becomes one to do now, its other ways to end it too. etape is its rank in the quest (from 1), texte what it says. Sent after quetes.debut for the first steps, then after the quetes.etape that opened it, and again when the step starts over (an "Also fails if" row set to recommence) or the quest resumes at it (quetes.reprise); its own annonce_debut event follows. Not sent again when a saved game is loaded. groupe (Journal de quêtes 8): true for a quest of the whole group.
quetes.etape_echec quete (text), etape (number), alors (text), manquee (boolean?), groupe (boolean?) A step of a quest failed (Journal de quêtes 11, events version 33): one of its "Also fails if" rows happened. etape is its rank in the quest (from 1), alors what that row does: echoue (the quest fails, quetes.echec follows), recommence (the step starts again from zero) or etiquette (the quest goes to the row's label). Also sent for an "Also fails if" row under the quest itself that does not make it fail (recommence: the quest starts again; etiquette), etape being then the step it was waiting for (0: none). For an optional step, manquee is true: the step is only missed, the quest goes on without it. groupe: true for a quest of the whole group.
quetes.reprise quete (text), etape (number), groupe (boolean?) A quest resumes (Journal de quêtes 11, events version 33): tried again after a failure (the log's Try again button, reessayer auto, quetes.reessayer, or given again when it does not start over), or started again by an "Also fails if" row of the quest set to recommence. etape is the step it resumes at (from 1; 0: none). The steps before it stay done; marks stay set and the world is not put back as it was: a Trigger rule on the step's annonce_debut does that. groupe: true for a quest of the whole group.
quetes.fin quete (text), xp (number?), piste (text?), groupe (boolean?), facultatives (table?) A quest is finished: all its steps are done. Its reward, if any, has been asked to the inventory. xp and piste (version 28): its XP for the Progression module and the track that gets it (empty: the main one). facultatives (Journal de quêtes 11): {faites, total}, how many of its optional steps were done, out of how many; absent when it has none. groupe (Journal de quêtes 8): true for a quest of the whole group (game setting groupe), sent once for the group, joueur being who moved it on.
quetes.echec quete (text), groupe (boolean?) A quest failed: an event, a mark, its time ran out, or quetes.echouer. It gives no reward; it may be given again if its settings allow it. groupe (Journal de quêtes 8): true for a quest of the whole group (game setting groupe), sent once for the group, joueur being who moved it on.
quetes.abandon quete (text), groupe (boolean?) The player abandoned a quest from the log (or quetes.abandonner asked for it). It gives no reward; it may be given again if its settings allow it. groupe (Journal de quêtes 8): true for a quest of the whole group (game setting groupe), sent once for the group, joueur being who moved it on.
accompagner.suivre cible (id?), qui (id?), lieu (id?) Ask a character carrying the Companion system to follow someone (Companion module, version 34): qui, a player or an object (missing: the one it followed last, else the host); lieu, a place where it announces accompagner.arrive once it gets there while following. cible: the character, by id or name; missing, every character carrying the system in the scene. "Follow me" after accompagner.attendre.
accompagner.aller cible (id?), lieu (id?), qui (id?) Ask a Companion character to walk to a place (lieu, an object or a point; missing: its setting destination), waiting whenever the player qui is too far; there it announces accompagner.arrive and waits. cible: as for accompagner.suivre.
accompagner.fuir cible (id?), de (id?), distance (number?), qui (id?) Ask a Companion character to run away from de (an object or a point; missing: qui, else the nearest player) until distance metres (missing: its setting distance_fuite); it announces accompagner.arrive once safe, and runs again if approached. cible: as for accompagner.suivre.
accompagner.attendre cible (id?) Ask a Companion character to wait where it is ("Wait here"), remembering whom it followed: accompagner.suivre without qui makes it follow them again. cible: as for accompagner.suivre.
accompagner.arreter cible (id?) Ask a Companion character to stop whatever it does and forget whom it followed. cible: as for accompagner.suivre.
sante.blesser cible (id), montant (number), source (id?) Ask the health module to remove health from an object.
sante.mort cible (id), source (id?) An object's health reached zero. What happens next belongs to the game: the health module does nothing else, unless its setting says to destroy the object.
dialogue.fin cible (id) A dialogue window closed: after its last line, or a choice leading to fin.
vehicule.monte cible (id), qui (id), joueur (id?) A player got into a vehicle (an object carrying the vehicule system) and now drives it: qui and joueur are their character (the host's, or a guest's). Announced by the engine.
vehicule.descendu cible (id), qui (id), joueur (id?) A player got out of a vehicle. Announced by the engine.
population.laisser cible (id), joueur (id?) Tell the Population module that a player left this object born of the population (put down, dropped, abandoned): it is kept as it is for its setting garder_laisse (30 minutes of play by default, 0: always), at most laisse_max per player, and saved. Ignored for an object the author placed (the world already remembers it). Getting out of a car born of the population (vehicule.descendu) does it by itself.
jour_nuit.heure heure (number), jour (number) A new hour of the world started (0 to 23), on day jour (1 the first day).
population.danger lieu (vector), rayon (number?), force (number?), sorte (text?), source (id?) Tell the Population module that something dangerous happens here (a shot, an explosion, a fight a game wants to signal): pedestrians within rayon (its setting peur_rayon when missing) flee, cars heading there stop, nobody appears there while the fear lasts. force (1 by default) multiplies the share who flee and how long. Gunshots (tir.tir), explosions (tir.explosion), impacts (projectile.touche) and deaths (sante.mort) do it by themselves. A game script can replace the reaction with game.override("population.reagir", function(default, e) ... end).
tir.tir source (id), arme (text), origine (vector), direction (vector) The player fired a ranged weapon (version 25), whether it hits or not: origine is the muzzle, direction the aim before spread. For a sound, a noise that alerts enemies, a counter.
tir.explosion source (id), arme (text), point (vector), rayon (number) A shot of the player exploded (version 25): a grenade at the end of its fuse or a rocket on impact. Health was removed within rayon metres, with no wall in between.
projectile.touche projectile (number), owner (id?), cible (id?), point (vector), normale (vector), partie (text?), os (text?), vitesse (vector), eau (boolean?), data (table?) A projectile launched by game.projectile hit something: cible is the object hit (nil for the terrain and for water), partie the part of a character's body (tete, torse, bras, jambes; missing for an object), os the bone, vitesse its velocity on arrival (m/s), data the table given at launch. eau = true when it entered water (point on the surface): it goes on, strongly slowed, and sinks (put a splash there, game.effect("eclaboussure", e.point), or stop it with game.stop_projectile). Announced by the engine; a projectile that bounces announces each hit.

Population (population)¶

Pedestrians who appear on the city pavements around the player, out of sight, walk along the streets and leave behind them. Use it whenever a city must feel alive: never write pedestrians in the scene, never animate them with timelines or scripts. The scene holds only these settings; the engine (engine/game/game_population.cpp) does the work at the start of each step. Never edit the module: set its values (modules_set).

Filling a city, step by step¶

  1. The city needs its lanes: a generated city writes its pavements, lanes and parking bays in voies.json. With none, the report's probleme says so and nobody appears: generate or regenerate the city first, never write pedestrians in the scene.
  2. Add the module if the game has none (modules_add population), then set it with modules_set. Never edit the module, its script or its module.json.
  3. The models: place hidden model objects in the scene and list them in modeles (pedestrians), modeles_voitures (driving cars, wheels as children) and garees_modeles (parked cars). Only models whose licence lets the game ship them: the mannequin shipped with the editor, CC0 or CC-BY models (with their credit), the free cars and passers-by of a pack. Never a paid pack's model in a shipped game, and never change a model (its mesh, size or materials): measure its bounds, the director reads them.
  4. How many, overall: curseur (0 nobody, 1 the set density, 2 twice) for everything at once; passants_100m for pedestrians, voitures_100m for driving cars, garees for the share of parking bays taken. voitures (unticked) removes the driving cars.
  5. Where (zones): zones, one row per place (lieu: a city district name, a map sector name or id, or partout; passants, voitures, garees, heures). A generated city already gives each district its values; write a row only to change one district (empty a park, fill a market street).
  6. When (hours): heures (ville, ville_nuit, village, constant, or 24 numbers of your own) with selon_heure ticked to follow the hour of the sky or of the Day and night module.
  7. Keep a character (a quest giver met in the street, a witness): name it in a quest (it is kept until the quest ends), or send population.garder {cible} (and population.liberer to give it back); see "Who is never taken back".
  8. Change how the street reacts to a danger: game.override("population.reagir", ...), or send population.danger from a script or a Trigger rule; to empty a place, set its zones row; see "Reactions".
  9. Before raising a density, estimate it (city_perf with {"population": {...}}, see "Lightening on your own"); after, prove it with game_run (see "Proving it").

How it works¶

  • Where: the pavements (trottoirs) of the city lanes (voies.json of each tile, the graph of game.route). A game with no generated city has no pavement yet: nobody appears.
  • Who: copies of the model characters in modeles (hidden objects of the scene; usually the animated mannequin animations/quaternius-ual1/UAL1_Standard.glb shipped with the editor, placed hidden). No row: every object tagged population.passant. Each copy plays its model's own walk clip (the clip whose name says walk, a loop first). Never make up a pose.
  • On arrival (the scene loads, or the player jumps further than rayon_min in one step): the whole disc up to rayon_max fills at once, in view too, before the first image. The pool of copies is prepared when the scene loads, not during play.
  • How many: passants_100m (6) per 100 m of pavement within rayon_max of the player, times curseur (1), at most budget (250) at once.
  • Where and when (zones and hours): each piece of pavement takes the density of the zone it lies in, times the coefficient of the hour, times curseur. heures is the overall hour model (ville: low at 4 am, peaks at 8 am and 6 pm; ville_nuit: the city that never sleeps; village: empty at night; constant); selon_heure (ticked) follows the hour of the realistic sky, or the jour_nuit.heure event of the Day and night module; no hour known: coefficient 1. zones (under Advanced) has one row per place: lieu (a city district name, a map sector name or id, or partout), passants (per 100 m of pavement), voitures (moving cars per km of lane: births and the director's target follow the zone where the car appears, 0 for none; hours do not change it) and garees (share of parking spaces taken, 0 to 1: each bay of the zone is drawn from the seed with it, 0 for none), both -1 for like elsewhere (version 6), heures (empty: like elsewhere). Any heures is a model name or 24 numbers of your own (0 h to 23 h, 0 to 4): the editor draws it as a 24 h curve whose points the user drags. A generated city writes its districts with the population block of their style (pedestrians, cars, parked share, hour model, varying with the district density) in its lanes: those are the defaults. A zones row naming a district replaces its values (one value shown in two places: the district panel writes the same row). To empty or fill a district, set its row; never write pedestrians.
  • The rings (Medium quality): nobody appears in sight. In sight means inside the camera's cone (its horizontal and vertical openings, each widened by marge_vue, 15 degrees) and not hidden: three rays from the camera to the pedestrian's head, waist and knees all hit a wall or a building to hide it. Further than rayon_min (35 m), out of the cone or hidden is enough; between rayon_cache (15 m) and rayon_min, only hidden behind the scenery; never within rayon_cache. They appear up to rayon_max (100 m), at most par_pas (4) per step; they leave beyond rayon_depart (120 m), or when out of sight for 4 s, further than 60 m and behind the player.
  • They walk along their pavement at vitesse (1.3 m/s, each a little faster or slower); at the end of a pavement they take another one that touches it, or turn back.
  • The copies come from a pool prepared once at the start (game.pool): no object is created during play. They carry the tags population and population.passant, and saves leave them out.

Cars (driving)¶

  • Where: the lanes of the city (voies and liaisons of voies.json), never a pavement. Each car follows its lane at the street's speed limit (the limite_vitesse signs of the city, in km/h or mph; vitesse_voiture, 13.9 m/s, on a street without a sign), keeps ecart_voitures (2.5 m) behind the car in front, stops at a red light of its junction (the feu lights: game.data(light).feu, the same cycle as their lamps), stops at amber when it can, gives way when it enters a roundabout (cede links) or turns left, never enters a junction while a car is on a crossing path or its exit is full, and picks one of its lane's links at each junction (straight, right, left, the left-turn lanes). No physics, except near the player (see Simulation levels).
  • Who: copies of modeles_voitures (hidden cars of the scene, with their wheels as children: the pool copies the whole group), or every object tagged population.voiture. Only redistributable cars (CC0, such as the free cars of Quaternius' Realistic Car Pack); never a paid pack model. Their length and width are read from their bounds.
  • How many: voitures_100m (1.5) per 100 m of lane within rayon_max_voitures, times curseur, at most budget_voitures (80). They appear between rayon_min_voitures (60 m; hidden behind a building, from 30 m) and rayon_max_voitures (200 m), out of sight, never on a junction nor within 12 m of another car on their lane; they leave beyond rayon_depart_voitures (240 m), or out of sight and behind the player. They carry the tags population and population.voiture.

Simulation levels (niveaux)¶

  • Near (closer than rayon_proche, 25 m; lost 5 m further): a pedestrian gets a capsule (game.character), walks to its place on its pavement by physics, steps aside from the player and the others (avoidance) and can be bumped; a driving car gets its model's collider (it can be hit) and brakes in front of the player or a near pedestrian (rayon_proche_voitures, 25 m).
  • On its line (further): as above, no physics. Out of sight (donnees, unticked by default; ticked): only its place moves on; the object is hidden and no longer placed, and it is placed before it shows again. Level changes never jump: the capsule starts where the pedestrian is shown, and one leaving the near level gives back its gap to the line slowly.
  • niveaux = false: everyone stays on its line (the behaviour before). The report gives niveaux {passants_proches, passants_en_donnees, voitures_proches, voitures_en_donnees, montees, descentes, bouscules, freinages, pire_saut_m}.

Who is never taken back (the important ones)¶

  • The director only ever takes back its own copies: anything the author placed in the scene (a quest giver, a guard, a shop keeper) is never touched.
  • A pedestrian leaves the director, stays itself and stops walking its pavement (an object like any other) as soon as: it carries the tag population.garder (game.add_tag, or the events population.garder / population.liberer {cible}); it goes with the player to the next scene (game.bring_along); a quest that has started names it (its donneur, or the fin_cible, fin_lieu, echec_cible, echec_lieu, reprise_lieu of any of its rows, and the values of a quest created from a template), until the quest ends, completed, failed or abandoned; Accompagner makes it follow, go, flee or wait (until accompagner.arreter); a player touches it: hurts it or kills it (sante.blesser, sante.mort with a player as source), talks to it (dialogue.fin), gets in it (vehicule.monte). Another one is born to keep the density.
  • When nothing names it any more, it goes back to the pool once the player is further than twice rayon_depart and it is out of sight. A quest object therefore persists until the end of its quest, then becomes ordinary again.
  • A script or a Trigger rule never writes these lists: set tags, send events. Name a pedestrian by its id (copies share their model's name). Engine calls behind the module: game.population_garder(reason, list), game.population_touche(target, player, how) and game.population_laisse(target, player, how).

What it remembers (the memory)¶

  • The short memory: a pedestrian who leaves out of sight is remembered memoire_courte (60) s. If the player comes back and the place is in the ring again, the same one comes back first (same model, same pavement, same direction), moved on by the time spent. After that, the next one there is another. The density still decides: a remembered pedestrian comes back only as the zone and the hour allow (always the same draw for the same one), and only while fewer are alive than the density asks for.
  • Traces: a parked car the game changed (colour, tags, size: dented, painted), a parked car pushed or taken from its bay (the bay stays empty, the bay reads its trace before its seed), a touched pedestrian, stay as they are when the player comes back, for traces_duree (600) s of play, at most traces_max (64), the oldest goes first.
  • What the player leaves (a car they got out of, vehicule.descendu; any object born of the population sent with population.laisser {cible, joueur?}) waits for them garder_laisse (1800) s of play, 0 = always, at most laisse_max (32) per player, never pushed out by other traces: they can change their mind and come back. Their last car is always kept.
  • Traces and what the player leaves go into the save with their age (game.save / game.load); the short memory does not.
  • Objects the author placed are not concerned: the world already remembers them.

Reactions (version 5)¶

  • A danger empties the street around it: a gunshot (tir.tir, at the muzzle), an explosion (tir.explosion: its radius is added to peur_rayon), a projectile impact (projectile.touche), a death (sante.mort), or population.danger {lieu, rayon?, force?, sorte?, source?} sent by any script or Trigger rule (a fight, a siren, a fire: anything a game wants to signal).
  • Within peur_rayon (30 m): a share peur_part (0.9, drawn from the seed) of the pedestrians flee along their pavement, away from the danger, peur_vitesse (2.5) times faster, with their own run clip if they have one (a clip named run, sprint, jog, course), otherwise their walk clip played faster; for peur_duree (20) s, then they walk on their line again, or leave out of sight like the others. Cars within the radius heading towards the danger stop where they are while the fear lasts; those driving away go on. Nobody appears within the radius while the fear lasts (the density drops there for a while). force multiplies the share and the duration; several dangers at the same place (under 2 m) extend the fear.
  • The important ones do not flee: a quest giver kept by its quest, a companion, a pedestrian touched by a player or tagged population.garder is no longer a pedestrian of the director. A game that wants them to flee sends accompagner.fuir.
  • To change the reaction, never edit the module: game.override("population.reagir", function(default, e) ... end) receives every danger e {lieu, rayon?, force?, sorte = tir, explosion, impact, mort or danger, source?}. Return without calling default to replace it (nobody flees: the game does its own thing), or call default(e) with changed fields (a larger rayon, a smaller force). Engine call behind it: game.population_danger(lieu, rayon?, force?).

Events¶

  • population.apparait {cible, sorte = passant or voiture}: a pedestrian or a car appears.
  • population.disparait {cible, sorte = passant or voiture}: it leaves (back to the pool). Never keep its id after this.
  • population.touche {cible, joueur, comment, sorte = passant}: a pedestrian becomes important (comment: frappe, tue, parle, monte, or what keeps it: quetes, accompagner, garder, emmene).
  • population.fuite {lieu, rayon, passants}: a danger made pedestrians flee (how many).
  • Listens to population.garder and population.liberer {cible}: put or remove the tag population.garder; to population.laisser {cible, joueur?} and vehicule.descendu: what the player leaves (above); to population.danger, tir.tir, tir.explosion, projectile.touche and sante.mort: the reactions (above).

Proving it (game_run)¶

After any change, run game_run with a walk through the city (the player moving and turning the camera) and read the report's population part. A proof says, with the numbers: naissances_vues = 0 and voitures.naissances_vues = 0 (nothing appeared in sight), plus_vivants within budget, and the cost (cout.mesure_ms next to cout.estime_ms, under cout.budget_ms). If the part has an alerte list, a rule is broken: say it to the user as it is, fix it (an alerte on cost: see "Lightening on your own"), and run again.

The report has a population part: heure (the hour used, -1 when unknown), zones (zones in use, city districts included), vivants (alive now), plus_vivants, cible (what the density asks for), naissances, departs, departs_derriere, refus_trop_pres and refus_dans_la_vue (places drawn then refused: too close, or in view), naissances_cachees (born closer or in the cone because hidden by the scenery), naissances_vues (births checked again in the exact camera image with free rays: it must stay 0), remplis and remplissages (pedestrians placed on arrival, and how many arrivals), reserve_ms and remplissage_ms (the pool and the arrival fill, outside the steps), naissance_la_plus_proche_m, importants (kept now), importants_touches, importants_devenus, importants_rendus, memoire_courte (pedestrians remembered now), revenus (came back the same), traces (ordinary traces now), traces_faites, laisses (what players left, kept now), trottoirs_m, modeles, reactions {dangers (received), dangers_vifs (still scaring now), fuyards (fleeing now), ont_fui (fled in all), voitures_arretees_pas (car steps held by fear), and the four peur_* values}, ms_par_pas, pire_ms, budget (the setting), niveaux (see Simulation levels), garees (the parked cars: part, cases, tirees, montrees, solides, plus_montrees, ms_par_pas, budget; also at the report's root as population_garees), cout {mesure_ms (the measured population step, parked cars included), estime_ms, estime_cpu_ms and estime_gpu_ms (the estimate of what lived at the busiest, same rates as city_perf), budget_ms (what the aimed machine allows, see below), qualite, images_s, and allegement when the cost is over the budget}, alerte (only when a rule is broken: a pedestrian or a car born in sight, more alive than a budget, a cost over the aimed machine's budget; one line each), and probleme when no model or no pavement was found. Its voitures part says the same for cars (vivantes, naissances, departs, naissances_vues...) plus arrets_au_feu (stops at a red light), arretees_au_feu (waiting at a red light now), attentes_carrefour (steps spent waiting to give way or for a free junction), ecart_min_m (the smallest bumper-to-bumper gap to the car in front: never under 2 m), ecart_gabarits_min_m (the smallest gap between the outlines of any two cars: above 0, no car goes through another), voies_m, feux (junctions with lights), its budget (the budget_voitures setting) and its own ms_par_pas and pire_ms.

Lightening on your own (the aimed machine)¶

You estimate the cost of a request and lighten it yourself; the user does not have to ask. Never touch the models (no simpler mesh, no smaller texture) and never edit the module: only its settings.

  • The aimed machine is the game's performance targets (world_set performanceTargets: quality and fps; by default Medium at 60 fps). The population may take a share of each frame: 4% in low (a weak machine), 9% in medium (1.5 ms at 60 fps), 12% in high, 15% in ultra. That is budget_ms in city_perf's population part and in game_run's cout. Change the targets only when the user names another machine or frame rate.
  • Before raising a density (or filling a new city), call city_perf with {"population": {...}} and the values you want. Its population part gives cout_estime_ms, budget_ms and au_dessus_du_budget; when it is over, allegement gives modules_set values (budget, budget_voitures, garees_allegement: a factor on top of every zone's and the overall garees, so it also thins districts that set their own) and their cout_estime_ms, under the budget (sous_le_budget). Write them with modules_set (keeping the user's density: the streets look as full near the player, fewer live at the busiest point), and tell the user what you lowered, the cost before and after, and why.
  • game_run does the same from what really lived: when cout.mesure_ms plus the drawing, or cout.estime_ms, is over budget_ms, its cout.allegement proposes the same settings.
  • If it is still too high, in this order: shorten the reach (rayon_max, rayon_max_voitures, garees_portee), keep niveaux ticked (only the near ones get physics) and tick donnees (out of sight, only the place moves on), then lower curseur last (it thins the whole city). Run city_perf, then game_run again, and give the numbers.
