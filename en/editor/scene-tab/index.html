The Scene tab¶

The first tab of the left column. You choose the place (the scene), then what it contains (its objects), like folders in File Explorer.

The game row¶

At the very top, with the globe icon, a row carries the game's title ("Jeu" at first; This game if the title is empty). On hover: "The game's settings: its sky, its window, its terrain". A click deselects everything, and the Properties then show the game's settings: its terrain, its sky and light, its window. See The properties.

Game scenes¶

In a project, the Game scenes section lists the places of your game: one scene per place (the outdoors, the inside of a house, a cave, a level). "One place per scene: a click opens it."

  • Open a scene: click its name. If the open scene has changes, the editor first asks whether to save them.
  • Create a scene: the + to the right of the title ("New scene: another place of the game (a house, a cave, a level)").
  • Start: the word shown to the right of the scene where the game starts. The exported game opens on it.
  • On hover, a scene shows its passages (which scene they lead to) and its arrival points.
  • Right-click a scene:
Command Effect
Open Opens this scene.
Rename Changes its name in place. The passages leading to it are updated in every scene.
Duplicate Creates a copy of the scene.
Delete Asks for confirmation, and lists the passages and scripts that name it and will lead nowhere. The scene is set aside in the project's work folder (.worldmint, "scènes supprimées"): nothing is lost. These cannot be deleted: the start scene, the one that holds the player's character, and the game's last scene.
Start the game here Makes it the start scene.

To link scenes together (a door that leads into a house), see Projects and scenes.

Sectors (large maps)¶

In a large world, the Sectors section cuts the map into named parts: the harbour, the old town, the north forest. They help you find your way, work on one part at a time, and lock what is finished. “One click: work on it alone.”

  • Create a sector: the + on the right of the title, then click and drag on the ground, in a view (preferably the top view, 7 on the numeric keypad). Its size shows while you draw; Escape cancels. You name the sector right away, like a new folder.
  • Work on a sector: click its name, in the list or on its label in the view. The object list, the selection, Ctrl+A and framing then only see it; the other objects are hidden in the views (nothing is deleted), and seen from above the rest of the map darkens. The line “Sector “Harbour”: 1,462 objects hidden around it” reminds you above the list. Whole map shows everything again.
  • The number on the right of a sector counts its objects: a building made of twenty pieces counts once.
  • The padlock locks a sector: neither you nor the agent can move, create, delete or paint anything in it any more, its ground included. A refused gesture goes back into place, and the console says “Refused: locked sector”. A second click unlocks it. Ctrl+Z stays free.
  • Double-click a sector: the view frames all of it.
  • Right-click: Work on this sector, Frame in the view, Rename, Lock or Unlock, Delete (its objects stay where they are).
  • The agent sees the same sectors: it works on the one you chose, and when it chooses one, the editor shows it (“The agent works on the sector…”). It never touches a locked sector.

An object belongs to the sector where it stands: a building, a copy of a reusable object, follows the place of its root; a folder used only for sorting shows with what it holds.

The scene's objects¶

Below the scenes, the Search the scene field filters the list: it then shows, flat, every object whose name contains what you type.

The list shows the objects of the open scene, arranged like folders:

  • The arrow (chevron) on the left opens or closes what an object contains; a double-click on the row does the same. The number on the right counts everything inside.
  • The icon tells the kind of object: model, basic shape, folder, played character, camera, arrival point (flag), terrain (globe), terrain region or water (a dot of its color), plant kind (leaf). A copy of a reusable object has the copy icon.
  • The gear marks an object that carries systems; on hover, their names ("Systems: ...").
  • A passage shows after its name the scene it leads to (→ House · Entrance), in red if it leads nowhere.
  • The eye on the right hides the object in the editor's view only ("Hide in the view, without changing the game"); a second click shows it again. View > Show everything again shows everything.
  • Objects with the same name ("Wall 1", "Wall 2", "Wall 3") are grouped under a "Wall" row with their count: a click on the row selects them all, the arrow shows them one by one. A forest of a hundred trees stays readable.
  • The plants of a plant kind are listed under it, with the word "plant": they are selected like objects. See The terrain.

Selecting¶

  • Click: selects the object. Ctrl + click adds it to the selection or removes it. Shift + click selects every object between the previous one and this one.
  • The selection in the list and in the 3D view is the same.

Arranging¶

  • Dragging an object onto another puts it inside: it becomes its child and follows it when it moves. The whole selection follows if the dragged object is part of it.
  • Dragging an object to the empty space below the list takes it out of its folder.
  • Group (Ctrl+G) puts the selection in a new folder; Ungroup (Ctrl+Shift+G) undoes the folder.
  • Dragging a system from the Systems folder of the library onto a row places it on that object (or on the whole selection). On the row of a same-name group, it creates a folder with that name, which holds the group and carries the system: you then set it once for all of them.

Right-click¶

On an object:

Command Shortcut Effect
Create inside Creates an object inside this one: Folder, Camera, Light, Arrival point, Zone, then the shapes Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Capsule, Plane.
Rename F2 Type the new name in place. Enter confirms, Esc cancels.
Duplicate Ctrl+D Copies the selection, with what it contains.
Copy Ctrl+C Copies the selection, to paste with Ctrl+V.
Make a reusable object Makes it a model to place many times: see The library.
Group Ctrl+G Puts the selection in a new folder.
Ungroup Ctrl+Shift+G Takes the folder's contents out and removes it.
Delete Del Deletes the selection. The played character is not deleted until another player is set.

In the empty space below the list, right-click offers the same objects to create, at the root of the scene.

What you can create¶

Object What it is
Folder Holds other objects; not seen in the game. A system placed on a folder applies to everything inside.
Camera A placed camera, which shows the game from where it stands: see The properties.
Light A placed light, a bulb or a spotlight, created a little above where you look: see Light and shadows.
Arrival point Where the player appears when arriving from another scene, facing the arrow. It is created where you are looking, on the ground.
Zone A box invisible in the game that carries the Zone system (activated if needed): it announces who enters and who leaves. A pressure plate, a trap, a finish line: see Physics and zones.
Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Capsule, Plane The basic shapes, to build without a model.
