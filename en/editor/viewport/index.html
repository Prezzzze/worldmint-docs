The 3D view¶

In the middle of the window: the scene, as the game will show it. You select objects there, move them, and sculpt the terrain.

Moving around the view¶

Gesture Effect
Hold the right button Orbit around what is under the mouse (in a front, top... view, the right button pans the view).
Ctrl + hold the right button Orbit around the selection, wherever the mouse is.
Hold the middle button Pan the view: what is under the mouse follows it.
Wheel Zoom, towards what is under the mouse, until you touch it.
F Frame the selection (nothing selected: the whole scene).
Home Show the whole scene: when you went too far, or are looking at the sky.
Numpad 7, 1, 3 Top, front, right view. With Ctrl: bottom, back, left.
Numpad 5 Back to perspective.
Q One view or four views.
Z Cycle Normal, Polygons, Wireframe.

Orbiting, panning and zooming start from what you point at: the speed follows its distance. The same mouse gesture moves the view a few centimetres right next to an object, and tens of metres above a 4 km map.

At the top left of the view, two lists choose what the view shows: Perspective, Top, Bottom, Front, Back, Left, Right; and the rendering: Normal, Polygons (faces and their edges), Wireframe (edges only). In Wireframe, the terrain reads like a map: a flat fill shaded by the slope and tinted by the altitude, with its faint edges, and its contour lines when you look at it from above (a stronger line every five; their spacing follows how far you are). It hides what is behind it: a large map does not drown your models. In the Top view, the contour lines also show in Normal: seen straight from above, the relief cannot be read otherwise. Next to them, the Show the whole scene button (the four corners) brings this view back to the whole scene.

From afar, the grid widens by ten (1 m, 10 m, 100 m...): its lines do not crowd into a blur. The Top, Front, Right... views have no fog: you see the whole map.

At the top right, the orientation ball shows the X (red), Y (green, up) and Z (blue) axes. A click on a dot shows the view from that side; dragging the ball turns the view.

Four views (Q key, or the four-squares button of the top bar) splits the view in four; the view under the mouse is the one the menus and shortcuts act on, outlined in the accent color. F and Home frame all four views at once.

Selecting objects¶

  • A click on an object selects it. For an object inside a folder: the first click takes the whole folder, the next one the piece you clicked, like a group of shapes in a slide.
  • Ctrl + click (or Shift + click) adds to the selection or removes from it.
  • Dragging in the view draws a rectangle: everything it touches is selected.
  • A click in empty space deselects everything.
  • On a terrain, the first click selects the Terrain, the next the region under the mouse. A plant is selected with a click, like an object.
  • A placed camera is selected by its symbol on screen.

The tool column¶

On the left of the view, a column of buttons.

Move, rotate, scale¶

Button Shortcut Effect
Move W The gizmo's arrows move the selection along an axis; the squares, in a plane.
Rotate E The rings turn the selection.
Scale R The handles change the size.

Several selected objects move together, around their center. In the top bar:

  • World axes / Object axes (the globe): the gizmo follows the scene's axes, or those of the turned object;
  • Snapping: moves by grid steps (the magnet): moves go by grid steps, and so do rotations and sizes. The steps are set in the Preferences.

Each gesture is undone with a single Ctrl+Z, however long.

Moving with the keyboard¶

To place an object to the centimetre, the arrow keys nudge the selection by one step (0.1 m by default, Arrow keys step in the Preferences); Shift nudges ten times further, Ctrl ten times less. The arrows follow the screen of the active view: left and right, and in perspective up and down move forward or back on the ground (in a front view, they move up and down). Page Up and Page Down always move up and down. Each press is undone with one Ctrl+Z; a key held down, with a single one.

Terrain tools¶

When the scene has a terrain, seven tools are added below a line. With a tool taken, the left click in the view sculpts or paints instead of selecting; a second click on the tool, the Stop sculpting (Esc) cross or Esc put it down.

Tool What it does
Hill Raises the ground under the brush, more at the center than at the edge.
Dig Digs the ground under the brush.
Flatten Brings the ground to the height where the stroke starts: a flat pad for a house.
Smooth Softens bumps and hollows.
Ramp Drag from one point to another: a path with an even slope, as wide as the brush.
Paint Gives a region (forest, sand...) to the ground under the brush.
Fill Click in a hollow: water fills it up to its rim and becomes a lake.

The tool taken shows an options bar at the bottom of the view:

  • Size: the brush radius, in metres (except for Fill);
  • Strength: for Hill, Dig, Flatten and Smooth;
  • Region: for Paint, the region to lay down;
  • the i recalls what the tool does. While sculpting, slopes too steep for the player show in red.

Each brush stroke is undone with Ctrl+Z.

Adding in the view¶

  • Drag a model from the library: it lands where you drop it, on the object or ground under the mouse.
  • Drag a system from the Systems folder of the library onto an object: it carries it. Dropped on the ground of a terrain, it goes on the Terrain and applies to all its regions; on a plant, it goes on that plant (or on all the selected plants).

What the view shows¶

  • The grid is hidden with View > Grid.
  • While models load, a banner says "Loading models: 3 of 10".
  • A selected camera shows, in a corner of the view, What this camera shows.
  • Objects hidden with the eye of the list are no longer seen in the view, but stay in the game.
