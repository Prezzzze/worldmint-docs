Particles and decals¶

Particles bring the world to life: a campfire, a torch, smoke, sparks, magic, rain or snow, and what goes with an action (wood chips when chopping a tree, dust when the player lands, a flash when a hit lands). Decals lay an image on the ground or a wall: a footprint, an impact, a scorch mark, a stain, a puddle, a sign.

Everything is set without code. The effects are generic: they depend on no model, and chips take the colour of what is hit, measured on the model itself.

An effect on an object¶

The Particle effect system goes on the object the effect comes from: a campfire, a torch, a cauldron. For an effect on its own (a fire in the middle of a camp, snow over a valley), Create > Particle effect places an invisible box that carries it: its settings come first in its properties, before its place and size. The effect already runs in the editor's view while you set it.

Its settings, in the object's properties:

  • Effect: fire (dancing flames, a brighter core, a warm glow, embers flying up and a little smoke), smoke, sparks, magic, rain, snow, dust, chips, explosion, hit impact.
  • Size: 1 for a campfire, 0.4 for a torch, 3 for a big blaze. For rain and snow, the width of the area where they fall.
  • Colour: the effect's own, or a chosen colour (a blue fire, green magic).
  • Amount: the number of particles, multiplied.
  • Looping: ticked, the effect runs as long as the object is there; unticked, it plays once when the game starts.
  • Duration and Height above the object's base (the flame at the tip of a torch).
  • Effect light and Light strength (fire, explosion, magic, sparks, hit): the light the effect gives around it, with its shadows, without them, or none (see the light of fires and effects).

Placed on a visible object, the effect stops while the object is hidden and comes back when it is shown. Place rain or snow on the player: it follows them everywhere.

Playing an effect with the Trigger¶

The Trigger has two actions:

  • Play an effect: on the target, the one it carries (its Particle effect system, lit again: a brazier lit by a lever), or a ready-made effect played once (an explosion, chips, magic), with its size.
  • Stop an effect: puts out the effect the target carries.

For example, a barrel that explodes when broken: When: It dies, Then: Play an effect, Explosion, size 2; then a rule that destroys it.

What the game already does¶

  • The player raises a little dust when landing after a jump or a fall (Player tab, Dust on landing, to turn it off).
  • An object that bursts or collapses makes chips in its colour and dust; an object that falls raises dust where it hits the ground.
  • Gathering sends chips flying at each hit, from the spot hit and in its colour (wood for a tree's trunk, stone for a rock): setting Chips at each hit.
  • Simple combat makes a flash, sparks and a little dust when a hit lands: setting A flash and sparks when a hit lands.

A decal¶

Create > Decal places a flat invisible box that carries the Decal system. Its image covers the box's width and depth, and lies on what is around its base: the ground, a floor, a rock. The box's scale sets the image's size; turning it around its height turns the image. Its settings come first in its properties.

  • Image: a stain, an impact, a scorch mark, a puddle, footprints, a skid mark (drawn by the engine), or an image of the project (PNG or JPEG, its transparency kept).
  • Opacity: from 0 (invisible) to 1.
  • Tint: white leaves the image as it is; dark red makes a blood stain, green a puddle of poison.

For a sign on a wall, tip the box so that its height points out of the wall, and place its base against it. The image lies on everything inside the box, characters included: keep it thin where people walk.

With a script¶

The agent can also write a script:

  • game.effect("explosion", position, {size = 2}) plays an effect at a place; game.effect("eclats", rock) on an object (its chips take its colour). With loop = true, the effect runs until game.stop_effect.
  • game.object_effect(fire, false) puts out the effect an object carries, true lights it again.
  • game.decal("impact", position, {size = 1.5}) places a decal; life = 10 fades it out after ten seconds.

The details are in the script reference.

Light on effects¶

Smoke, dust, chips, rain, snow and decals are lit like the rest of the scene: by the sun, darker in the shadow of a roof or a tree, and by the placed lights close by (smoke passing in front of a lantern takes its colour). Fire, sparks, magic, explosions and hit impacts make their own light: they stay just as bright at night, and light up what is around them, with shadows for fire, explosions and magic: a cube next to a campfire casts its shadow. Nothing to place next to them; the details are in the light of fires and effects.

The agent's test¶

Particles are simulated by the game itself: the same game gives exactly the same particles. When the agent tests the game, its report counts the effects played by name, the particles born and the most alive at once, and the decals placed; its captures show them.

In the editor's view¶

An effect that can be seen in a view animates on its own, even when you touch nothing (30 frames per second in the view you are working in, 10 in the others): you see the fire dance while you adjust it. When no effect can be seen any more (out of view, or put out), the editor goes back to rest, and so does the graphics card.

Effect quality levels¶

Effects follow the graphics quality, on three levels:

High Medium Low
Particles at once, at most 6,000 3,000 1,500
Particles given off all three quarters, a little larger half, a little larger
Light on smoke and dust the sun and its shadows, placed lights and their shadows without the placed lights' shadows the sun and the sky, no shadow
Largest size of a particle on screen 90% of the image height 70% 50%
Decals placed by the game, at once 128 96 48

An effect never breaks the game: beyond their number, new particles are not born, it only looks lighter. The same goes for decals placed during the game: beyond their limit, the oldest fades out to make room for the new one. The game started with --low-power takes Low; --particles low, medium or high chooses the effects' level on its own.

A particle brushing past the camera fades out instead of covering the whole screen, and none goes beyond its largest size on screen: walking through smoke does not slow the game down.
