Creating a move¶

A model doesn't always have the move you need: a wolf without a walk, a guard who doesn't wave, a missing attack. Create it in the tool: keyframes bone by bone in the timeline, played in a loop, saved as a move of the model. Systems, timelines and scripts then play it like the moves of the files.

Nothing is specific to a model pack: the skeleton is read from the model, and angles are given around the character's axes, as when adjusting a pose.

Creating a move¶

  1. Select the character in the scene (the player, a guard, an animal: any model with a skeleton).
  2. In its Properties, open Skeleton pose and click Create a move.
  3. Give it a name ("Walk", "Wave"), a length, say whether it is Looping, and choose what it starts from:
    • The rest pose: an empty move, to build;
    • An existing move: read as keyframes, ten per second, to correct it without touching the original file;
    • The adjustment layer: its pose becomes the first keyframe.
  4. The timeline opens at the bottom, on this move: one row per animated bone, grouped by body part (head, back and hips, arms, legs, other bones), each keyframe a diamond.

Setting keyframes¶

  1. Place the playhead: click or drag on the ruler of seconds, or click on a row.
  2. Click a bone, in the view, in the list of bones or on its row in the timeline.
  3. Turn it with the gizmo (E), or move its point (W): the hips move, a hand or a foot bends the arm or the leg, the head or the back lean. Or type its angles under Bone rotation (and, for the hips, Bone offset). With Auto keyframe (on at first), a keyframe is set at the playhead.
  4. Do it again at another moment. Space (the mouse over the timeline) plays the move in a loop.

Every gesture is undone with Ctrl+Z, even on several keyframes at once (with the mouse over the timeline, Delete, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V and Ctrl+A go to the keyframes, not to the objects of the scene).

Gesture What it does
Click a diamond Chooses this keyframe. On the row of a body part or on "All keyframes", all its bones at that moment.
Ctrl+click, Shift+click, rectangle As in File Explorer: Ctrl+click adds or removes a keyframe from the choice, Shift+click chooses everything since the last click on this row, a rectangle dragged in empty space chooses what it covers (with Ctrl or Shift, in addition). Ctrl+A chooses them all, Escape none.
Drag a diamond Moves all the chosen keyframes in time, together, by the same amount.
Right-click a row Keyframe here, Remove keyframes, Copy keyframes, Paste keyframes here, Interpolation, Copy the pose, Paste the pose.
Delete Removes the chosen keyframes.
Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V Copies the chosen keyframes, then pastes them at the playhead with their spacing (into another move too).
Interpolation For the chosen keyframes: linear (steady), smooth (slow at start and end), stepped (it jumps from one pose to the next).
Ctrl+wheel, Shift+wheel, Show all Zooms around the mouse, scrolls (or drag the bar under the tracks), then goes back to the whole length.
Keyframe Sets a keyframe of the chosen bone at the playhead, or of every animated bone if none is chosen.
Copy the pose, Paste the pose Copies the angles of every bone to another moment (the end of a step takes back its start).
Copy the left onto the right (or the reverse) The right side takes the left side's pose at that moment, seen in a mirror.
Mirror The bone on the other side follows each gesture, as in a mirror.
Close the loop Copies the first pose to the end. When looping, a bone with a keyframe at the start already goes back to it at the end.
Onion skin Shows the skeleton a little before and a little after, in pale lines.
Feet on the ground Turning the hips or the back leaves the feet where they are: the legs bend by themselves (to crouch, to lean).
Bone gizmo Turn (E) rotates the bone; Move (W) takes its point where you drag it (the Move and Rotate buttons of the toolbar do the same). A hand or a foot bends the two bones above it.
Bend direction For a moved hand or foot: the elbow or knee bends as in the model's rest pose, otherwise the elbow backward and the knee forward; Forward or Backward force it.
Move length, Looping, Move name Can be changed at any time. The same name as a move of the file replaces it everywhere.

Angles are given around the character's axes at rest (as for a pose adjustment): for a model facing forward, X raises a leg or an arm forward (negative angle) or backward, Y turns the bone on itself, Z tilts it sideways.

Example: a walk for a wolf¶

A walk cycle needs only a few keyframes. For a four-legged animal, at a trot, the legs go in diagonal pairs:

  1. Create "Wolf walk", 1 second, in a loop, from the rest pose.
  2. At 0 s: the front left and back right legs forward (negative X), the other two backward.
  3. At 0.5 s: the reverse (copy the pose of 0 s, paste it, then copy left onto right).
  4. At 0.25 s and 0.75 s: bend the lower part of the leg coming back forward, so it leaves the ground.
  5. Play it in a loop, tune it, then give "Wolf walk" to a system's animation setting (an enemy on patrol), or play it from a script: game.play(id, "Wolf walk").

Where the move goes¶

It is a file of the project, animations/<name>.wmanim, saved with it. It belongs to the model: every character using this model has it, like the moves of its file (the player in its animation graph, characters through their systems, timelines, scripts). An exported game takes it along. On another model, add it to the player's animation files: it is borrowed like a file, through the bone map.

Pose layers are added on top of this move too, in the game.

If the model moves¶

The move and the pose adjustments follow the path of their model, and an adjustment follows the name of the move it targets:

  • Rename or move the model in the library (right-click the project model: Rename, F2, or Move to; or drag it onto a folder): whatever uses it follows, the objects of every scene, its moves, its pose adjustments. Ctrl+Z puts the file back. A .gltf, .obj or .fbx, which keep files next to them, can only be renamed in place.
  • Rename the move (Move name): the pose adjustments that targeted it follow, in the same Ctrl+Z.
  • A link broken outside the editor (the model deleted or moved in File Explorer, a changed animation file): Skeleton pose says so in red, and offers Attach to this model, or Attach to a move for an adjustment.

With the agent¶

Just ask: "make a walk for the wolf". The agent reads the bones (pose_layers_get), creates the move (skeleton_animation_set), reads it back (skeleton_animation_get), looks at it frame by frame (view_capture with animation), in a single step that Ctrl+Z undoes. It never touches the model file.
