Light and shadows¶

A scene's light comes from the sun, the sky and the ground, and from the lights you place. It is set in the game settings: deselect everything (or click the game's line, at the very top of the Scene tab), Sky and light section.

The sun, the sky and the ground¶

Setting Effect
Sun color, Sun intensity The color and strength of its light. A white surface facing the sun takes exactly its color.
Sun direction Where it shines from (X, Y, Z): positive Y, it is up. Shadows go the opposite way.
Sky The color of the sky's light: it lights everything, even in the shade, and is reflected by what is smooth or metal.
Light from the ground The light bounced by the ground, which lights the underside of objects.
Exposure How much light reaches the image, as on a camera. With automatic exposure, it adds to what the eye chose.
Automatic exposure Like an eye getting used to it: when you walk into a dark place (a house lit by a small window, a cave), the image brightens over a second or two, and comes back when you walk out, without dazzling. In sunlight, the image does not change. On by default, also for scenes made before it.
Brighten up to How many times brighter the image can get in the dark: 1 never, 4 by default (a dark room becomes readable without turning into daylight).
Darken up to How many times darker it can get under a very strong light: 1 never (default: a sunny scene stays as you set it), 2 to 4 for a glare when walking out of a cave.
Adaptation time The time the eye takes to get used to the dark: 1.5 s by default; it gets used to light three times faster. 0: at once.

In the editor, the views show the image once the eye is used to it; you see it brighten little by little in the game. A passage to another scene or a teleport arrives already used to it, without a flash. Night stays night: with the day and night cycle (or a script that lowers the sun), the eye only brightens what is darker than the current daylight; a dark house at noon is brightened, nightfall is not. Scripts set it with game.set_environment (auto_exposure, auto_exposure_brighten, auto_exposure_darken, auto_exposure_seconds).

Sun shadows¶

Objects, characters, terrain and plants cast their shadow in the sun: on the ground, on the water and on each other, and also on the smoke, dust, rain or snow passing below them, and on decals. So does the object a character holds. Shadows are soft at their edges and don't flicker when the camera moves.

Setting Effect
Sun shadows Unticked: no sun shadows, lighter rendering.
Shadow quality For the sun and the lights (fires included). Depends on the computer: low on an integrated graphics chip (most laptops), high on a graphics card. High: sharp all the way. Medium: one shadow slice less in the distance, fewer lights with shadows. Low: blurrier up close, lighter to draw. Off: no shadows at all, from the sun or the lights, the lightest. In the editor, it never goes above the View quality (Preferences); the game follows it. The player can also choose their own in the game menu (Shadows row).
Shadow distance How far around the camera they are drawn (150 m by default). Beyond that, they fade out gently. The further it is, the less sharp they are up close: for a game seen up close (a dungeon), 50 to 100 m is enough.
Shadow softness Softens their edge as under a real sun: the shadow stays sharp at the foot of an object and gets softer further from it (a pole's shadow, sharp at its base, blurred at its end). It is the size of the sun in the sky, in degrees: 0 sharp (default), 0.5 the real sun, 2 to 4 a hazy sky, up to 10 very soft.

Shadows also show in the editor's view, except in wireframe only.

If the game is slow on a laptop

Lower the Shadow distance, or set Shadow quality to Low, or Off as a last resort. On an integrated chip, Depends on the computer already picks Low. Shadow softness costs a little more in High and Medium (the penumbra is searched at each point); in Low, it is only a fixed blur, almost free.

Fog and glow¶

In the distance, the scene always fades gently into the Background color instead of being cut off: that is the horizon fog, with no setting. For more, in Sky and light:

Setting Effect
Fog distance A fog over the whole scene: at this distance, it hides almost everything in its color. 0: none. A light mist: 300 to 800 m; a thick fog: 40 to 100 m. It also hides what is too far to be drawn.
Fog color The color of what is in the fog, and of the sky behind it.
Fog altitude, Thins out with height A fog that stays low (a valley, a swamp, the mist over the sea): at its altitude it has the set thickness, below it gets thicker, above it halves every so many meters (5 to 30). 0: the same everywhere.
Glow of bright lights A soft glow around what shines very brightly: a material giving off light, the sun shining on water or metal, a lamp close by. 1 by default, discreet; 0: none. Sand or snow in full sun don't get it.

Volumetric fog: rays of light¶

Turn on Volumetric fog (in Sky and light) so that sunlight shows in the air, with the shadow of whatever hides it: rays between trees and through windows, a darker mist under the leaves, a brighter one when looking toward the sun. The fog keeps its Distance and Color (it hides as much as before); it also works without fog, thanks to the air.

Setting Effect
Rays in the air A slightly hazy air that shows the rays even without fog. 0: none; 0.2 by default, mostly when looking toward the sun; 1: very strong, from the side too. In a room, a little fog (dust) shows the rays from the windows.
Scattering toward the sun How much brighter the mist shines when looking toward the sun. 0: the same everywhere; 0.3 by default; 0.6 to 0.9: a strong glow around the sun, bright rays against the light.

Rays need a sun low enough to pass between things (a Sun direction whose height, the second value, is small), and something that casts shadows: trees, walls. With ray tracing, the shadow in the mist comes from rays, through the gaps in the leaves; without it, from shadow maps: the same look.

Depending on the computer

In Low quality (an integrated chip), the fog stays the usual one, without its rays. In Medium (the editor's views) and High, it is light: a few hundredths of a millisecond per frame on a recent graphics card, the same whatever the image size.

Fog in one area only: the fog volume¶

The fog of Sky and light covers the whole map. For a mist that stays in its place (a valley floor, an undergrowth, a marsh, the smoke of a cave), choose Create > Fog volume: an invisible box that the fog fills, and that fades out towards its edges. Move it and scale it like an object; several volumes can overlap. Its settings are those of the Fog volume system: its Visibility (at this distance you can hardly see anything), its Color, its Soft edges, and Thicker at the bottom for a mist that stays on the ground.

With volumetric fog on, the volume catches the sun's rays and shadows: a volume in a forest, with no fog in Sky and light (Distance at 0), shows rays there only. In Low quality, and far from the camera (beyond the shadow distance), it is drawn without its shadows, but always in its place.

Sun rays without mist: the glowing air¶

For sun rays in a forest or a room, like dust lit by the sun, without a veil over the rest, choose Create > Glowing air (or, in a fog volume's properties, Kind: Glowing air (rays only)). It is an almost invisible air: it only shows where the sun crosses it, mostly when looking toward the sun, and the air in the shade stays clear. Its Air visibility sets how strong the rays are (200 m by default; 100 m for strong rays, 400 m for a light trace); it has no colour (it takes the sun's). It needs volumetric fog on, in High or Medium quality, and a sun low enough to pass between foliage or walls; in Low quality it does not show.

You want Choose
Sun rays in the forest, through a window Glowing air
The mist of a valley, a marsh, smoke Fog volume

Placed lights¶

A lamp, a torch, a street lamp, a lit window, a spotlight on a stage: Create > Light (in the scene list). It appears a little above where you look; move it like an object.

  • A Bulb lights all around it; a Spotlight lights in a cone, toward the bottom of the object: turn it to aim.
  • Its Color is the object's. Its Intensity is its strength at 1 m: it falls off with distance, and fades out gently at its Range.
  • There can be many: each point of the image only computes the lights that reach it. Keep ranges short (a room: 5 to 10 m), that is what costs.
  • Light's shadows: what it lights casts its shadow. Only a few lights at a time get them, the closest to the camera (six spotlights, or one bulb, in low quality; twice as many in high). Keep them for the ones that matter.
  • Shadow softness (with its shadows): the size of what gives the light, in meters. 0 sharp, 0.05 a bare bulb, 0.3 a lantern or a globe. As in the sun, the shadow stays sharp at the foot of an object and gets softer further from it.
  • For them to show, it takes a bit of night: lower the Sun intensity and darken the Sky and the Light from the ground.
  • They light everything around them: objects and terrain, but also water (a lamp at the edge of a lake is reflected in it), smoke, dust, rain and snow passing near them, and decals. Fire, sparks and magic make their own light: lights don't change them.

The light of fires and effects¶

A fire, an explosion, magic, sparks or the flash of a hit light up what is around them: each carries its own light, with nothing to place next to it. It follows the effect, takes its colour (a blue fire lights in blue), grows with its Size, comes on with it and goes out after its last flames. A fire's light flickers and moves a little with the flames; an explosion's is a flash that dies out in less than a second; magic's sways slowly.

Fire, explosions and magic cast shadows: a cube next to a campfire casts its shadow on the ground and on the wall behind it, as soft as the width of the flames. Sparks and hits, too brief, have none. The object carrying the effect (the logs of a campfire, the head of a torch) casts no shadow from its own light.

In the effect's properties (Particle effect system):

  • Effect light: With its shadows (default), Without shadows (the light alone, lighter), or None (the effect is only drawn).
  • Light strength: multiplied; 0.5 for a discreet glow, 2 for a blaze that lights the whole camp.

These lights count with the placed lights: the closest to the camera first, within the same limit (and the same shadow limit). They follow the Shadow quality, show in the editor's view while you set the effect, and change nothing in the game: only the image.

In the game, the Trigger switches them on, off (Switch a light on, Switch a light off, in turn) or changes their color: a lever lighting a lamp, an alarm turning red. Scripts do it with game.light and game.set_light (see the scripts reference). Hiding a light switches it off too.

When the game saves, the lights go into the save: a lamp switched on or off, recoloured or made brighter or dimmer during the game comes back as it was when loading, with nothing to write (see Saved games).

Rendering quality¶

At startup, WorldMint picks a rendering quality for the computer: Low on an integrated graphics chip, High on a graphics card. Settings set to Depends on the computer follow it. To try the other one, start the game with --qualite basse or --qualite haute.

It also sets the terrain: in Low, the relief is simplified a little sooner in the distance, a large map's detail loads up to 500 m instead of 1 km, and plants stop at 60 % of their distance (see The terrain).
