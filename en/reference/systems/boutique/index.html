Shop¶

A merchant who sells when used: a window with their items and prices, the player's money, and Buy and Sell buttons. The money is a kind of inventory item, such as "Gold".
Identifier boutique
Version 4
Placed on objects yes
Also activates Interaction, Inventory
Also needs, on the same object Interaction

Events¶

The descriptions come from the code.

What it announces¶

Event Fields Description
boutique.achat cible (id), objet (text), quantite (number), prix (number), monnaie (text) The player bought an item in a shop: cible is the merchant, quantite how many they received, prix what they paid, in monnaie (a kind of inventory item). The inventory has already changed.
boutique.vente cible (id), objet (text), quantite (number), prix (number), monnaie (text) The player sold an item to a merchant (cible): quantite how many they gave, prix what they received, in monnaie. The inventory has already changed.
inventaire.ajouter objet (text), quantite (number), joueur (id?) Ask the inventory to add items, such as a reward. Answered by inventaire.change, or inventaire.plein. With several players, joueur says whose bag; without it, the player whose action is being handled, or every player.
inventaire.retirer objet (text), quantite (number) Ask the inventory to remove items, such as a payment. Answered by inventaire.change, or inventaire.manque if there are not enough.

What it listens to¶

Event Fields Description
interaction.utiliser cible (id), joueur (id) The player used the object in reach: a door, a merchant, an item to pick up. Whoever owns that object decides what it does.
boutique.ouvrir cible (id) Ask the shop system to open the shop of cible, as if the player had used it: from a dialogue answer, a trigger, a script.
inventaire.change objet (text), quantite (number), total (number), chargement (boolean?) The quantity of an item changed. total is the new quantity. chargement is true when a saved game gives the items back (game.load): nothing was picked up.
inventaire.plein objet (text) An item could not be added: every slot holds another kind of item.
inventaire.manque objet (text), quantite (number) Items could not be removed: there are not enough.
hud.clic cible (text), joueur (id?) A button of the HUD was clicked: pressed, then released on it; or chosen with A (Enter) while it has the gamepad or keyboard focus; or the window's back button (back = true), with B (Escape). cible is the button's key (game.hud_button), joueur the player who clicked (a guest clicks on their own computer; the host plays the click). Announced by the engine, before the scripts' on_update of that step.

Points a script can replace (game.override)¶

The descriptions come from the code.

  • boutique.texte: function(default, key) -> the text the shop shows for that key: titre_vide, bourse, acheter, vendre, prix, reste, epuise, plus_en_stock, fermer, achete, vendu, manque, place, sans_objet. It may hold {objet}, {n} and {monnaie}, replaced afterwards (the item and the money in the player's language). The default is the game's translation of the text in the original language (translations_get lists them as built-in texts of the shop); without one, French or English, after the player's language.

Shop (boutique)¶

A merchant who sells: when the player uses them (E), a window lists their items with prices, the player's money, and Buy and Sell buttons. The money is a kind of inventory item, such as "Or". Activating it activates interaction and inventaire; placing it on an object places interaction there too. Use it for every shop the user asks for (a merchant, a blacksmith, a vending machine) rather than a script.

Place it on the merchant with systems_attach, then set its values with systems_set:

  • articles: the list of what they sell, one row each: objet (the kind of item, as the inventory stores it: "Épée"), a_vendre (on: it can be bought; off: the merchant only buys it back), quantite (how many one purchase gives: 10 arrows), prix, stock (purchases before running out; 0: no limit), rachat (what the merchant pays when the player sells one back; 0: they buy none, and no Sell button shows).
  • monnaie: the kind of item used to pay ("Or" by default). Name it, and the items, in the user's language, exactly as the inventory stores them. The player gets money like any item: coins to pick up (inventaire with quantite 50), a quest reward, inventaire.ajouter.
  • nom: the window's title (empty: the object's name). Set the interaction prompt texte to "Shop" or "Trade" in the user's language.
  • a_l_usage (on by default): the shop opens when the merchant is used. A merchant who also carries dialogue talks first, and nothing has to be turned off: the shop waits, and the dialogue's last line offers a "Show me your goods" button ("Voir la marchandise" in French) that closes the conversation and opens the shop. To offer it elsewhere in the conversation, give an answer annonce: "boutique.ouvrir" and suite: "fin" (the answer's event carries the merchant as cible): the dialogue then adds no button of its own. Turn a_l_usage off only for a shop opened by a zone or a script.

Buying takes the price (inventaire.retirer), then gives the item (inventaire.ajouter). The window says it clearly when the player lacks money ("You need 5 more Gold"), when the inventory is full (they get their money back), or when an item is sold out. Selling does the reverse, one item at a time, and refills a limited stock.

Example, a blacksmith who sells a unique sword and potions, and buys back wolf pelts:

{"monnaie": "Or", "articles": [{"objet": "Épée", "prix": 30, "stock": 1}, {"objet": "Potion", "quantite": 2, "prix": 5}, {"objet": "Peau de loup", "a_vendre": false, "rachat": 4}]}.

Other details:

  • Events: boutique.achat and boutique.vente (cible the merchant, objet, quantite, prix, monnaie), after the inventory changed: for a sound, a quest step ("buy a sword": a quetes step on boutique.achat), a declencheur rule. game.emit("boutique.ouvrir", {cible = id}) opens a merchant's shop from a zone or a script.
  • Game settings: pause (the world stops while shopping, on by default), largeur (the window's width, a share of the screen).
  • Texts follow the player's language. The shop's own texts (Buy, Close...) are built-in texts of the system, listed by translations_get with the game's texts: translate them there for every language the game offers (French and English are built in). The shop's name (nom), the money and the items are translated with the game's tables too; events keep their original names. To change a word, override boutique.texte from a game script: game.override("boutique.texte", function(default, key) if key == "acheter" then return "Prendre" end return default(key) end). Keys and their {n}, {objet}, {monnaie} placeholders are in modules_info.
  • HUD keys start with boutique.: do not use that prefix in a game script.
  • Saved games: game.save keeps what each merchant has left of a limited stock, and game.load gives it back. Nothing to write.
  • Gamepad and keyboard: the first Buy button has the focus; the d-pad or the arrows move it, A or Enter buys, B or Escape closes (boutique.fermer is the window's back button).
  • Testing: in game_run, give money first (inventaire.ajouter), walk to the merchant, press interact, then click boutique.acheter.<row> (or boutique.vendre.<row>, boutique.fermer); the report's hud gives each button's area and boutique.message what the shop answered. With a gamepad: segments with pad (["pad_down"], then ["pad_a"], each followed by a segment without); the report marks the button with the focus (focused).

Several players¶

One copy per player: each player has their own window and money (their own inventory). A merchant's limited stocks are shared: what one buys is gone for the others. The host's saved game keeps the stocks.
