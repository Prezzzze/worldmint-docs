Ragdoll¶

An animated character that falls limp, like a rag doll: when it dies, under a hard hit, or when the Trigger asks. Its body is built on its own, from its skeleton. It can get back up, and its animation resumes smoothly.
Identifier chiffon
Version 2
Placed on objects yes

Settings for the whole game¶

In the Game tab, Systems section, by opening the system's card.

None.

Settings on each object¶

In the Properties of an object or folder that carries the system, Systems section. A folder passes its values on to what it contains; an object can change a value for itself only.

Setting What it does Name in code Type Default Limits or choices
Falls limp when it dies (Health system) When its health reaches zero, it collapses like a rag doll, and does not get back up. a_la_mort Yes or no yes
Falls under a hit harder than (m/s, 0: never) An object that hits it and gives it more than this speed, in meters per second, knocks it down: a rolling rock, a thrown barrel. 6: a clear hit. 0: never. choc Number 6 from 0 to 100
Gets back up after a hit Knocked down by a hit (not by dying), it gets back up after a while, and its animation resumes smoothly. se_relever Yes or no yes
Gets up after (s) How many seconds it stays down before getting up, once still. delai Number 3 from 0.2 to 60
Mass (kg) The weight of the falling body, in kilograms, shared among its limbs by their size: 70 for an adult, 25 for a child, 300 for an ogre. masse Number 70 from 1 to 10 000
Limb looseness (degrees) How far each limb can bend relative to the one carrying it, in degrees: 45 is natural, 20 falls stiff like a statue, 90 is very floppy. raideur Number 45 from 5 to 120
Floats in water Fallen into water, the body floats, the head and shoulders just above the water, and drifts with it. Otherwise, it sinks slowly to the bottom, like a knight in armor. flotte Yes or no yes

What it announces¶

Event Fields Description
chiffon.tombe cible (id) A character fell limp as a ragdoll: on its death, under a hard hit, or by game.ragdoll. Announced by the engine.
chiffon.releve cible (id) A ragdoll got back up: its animation resumes. Announced by the engine.

What it listens to¶

Event Fields Description
sante.mort cible (id), source (id?) An object's health reached zero. What happens next belongs to the game: the health module does nothing else, unless its setting says to destroy the object.

Ragdoll (chiffon)¶

An animated character falls limp like a rag doll, then can get back up and its animation resumes smoothly. Its body is built by the engine from its skeleton (capsules measured on its bones, joints with reasonable limits): nothing to set per model, any skeleton works.

  • systems_attach it on the characters (the player, enemies, villagers) or their folder. Per object: a_la_mort (falls when its sante reaches zero, and stays down), choc (a hit from a moving object faster than this, in m/s, knocks it down; 0 never), se_relever and delai (after a hit, it gets back up once still for that many seconds), masse (kg), raideur (how far each limb bends, in degrees: 45 natural, 20 stiff).
  • In water, the fallen body floats, head and shoulders just above the surface, and drifts; set « flotte » to false to make it sink (a knight in armor). The game_run « water » part lists it.
  • The character must be animated (a model with bones and animations): a static model cannot fall as a ragdoll.
  • Without code: a declencheur rule with action chiffon (falls) or relever (gets up), cible the character.
  • Scripts: game.ragdoll(id, true) makes it fall (an optional third argument, a vec, throws it), game.ragdoll(id, false) gets it back up, game.ragdolled(id) tells if it is down. Events chiffon.tombe and chiffon.releve (cible).
  • While down, the player does not move and the camera follows the body; a character is not walked by its scripts.
  • Check with game_run: ragdolls gives each one's falls, get_ups, and whether it is down at the end.

Several players¶

One for the whole game: any character carrying it falls on death, guests' characters included (on the host; guests see their place, not the limp pose).
