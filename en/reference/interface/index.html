Every editor control¶

Every field, section, button and menu of the editor, area by area, with what it does. It is the text shown as a tooltip when you rest the mouse on it in the editor. The settings of game systems are described on each system's page.

Adjusting a character's pose¶

Explained in detail: Adjusting a character's pose.

Control What it does
Add a joint here Adds a new joint, child of the chosen one, a little beyond it: you name it, then place it with the gizmo. Without skin it is an attach point (a weapon, a held object, particles); with « Take the skin around » it bends the part of the model beyond it. It can then be animated like the others.
Adjust the pose Shows the skeleton in the view and the list of its bones. Click a bone, then turn it (E) or move its point (W) with the gizmo, or type its angles: the character changes before your eyes.
Adjusted move The move the adjustment is added to: one move, every move, or "Seated on a vehicle", the pose the game gives the driver of a motorbike or a car.
Adjustment layer A layer groups turned bones, for one move or for all of them. A model can have several ("Hands on the handlebar", "Hunched back"). The first adjustment creates one.
Adjustment strength At 100%, bones turn by their full angle; at 50%, by half; at 0%, the adjustment does nothing.
Back to the original skeleton Removes every correction of this model's skeleton: moved, turned, removed and added joints. Ctrl+Z brings them back.
Bend direction When moving a hand or a foot: which way the elbow or knee bends. Automatic follows the model's rest pose if it is bent there, otherwise the elbow backward and the knee forward. Forward or backward force it, for a limb that is straight at rest or an animal's leg.
Bone gizmo What the gizmo does on the chosen bone: Turn (E) rotates it; Move (W) takes its point where you drag it. The hips really move; a hand or a foot bends the two bones above it (the arm, the leg) without stretching them; an elbow, a knee, the head or the back turn the bones that carry them. The Move and Rotate buttons of the toolbar do the same.
Bone rotation The angles added to this bone, in degrees, around the standing character's axes: X tilts it forward or back, Y turns it around, Z tilts it sideways.
Copy the left onto the right Gives the right side the left side's adjustments, mirrored.
Copy the pose Keeps this layer's angles to paste them elsewhere: in another layer, or on another character.
Copy the right onto the left Gives the left side the right side's adjustments, mirrored.
Correct the skeleton Correct the skeleton itself, not a move: put a joint back where the limb should bend (a knee behind the leg, an elbow inside the arm, common on an automatically rigged skeleton), turn it, remove it or add one. The model does not move: only its rest pose changes, and all its moves then bend around the new joint. The model file is never changed: the correction is stored in the project, for this model.
Delete the layer Removes the layer and all its adjustments. Ctrl+Z brings it back.
Finish adjusting Hides the skeleton and gives back the object's gizmo. The adjustments stay saved.
Joint name The new joint's name, as scripts, held objects and the agent will refer to it. It must differ from the skeleton's names.
Joint offset How far this joint was moved inside the model at rest, along the character's axes (X to its left, Y up, Z forward), in model units. Drag or type; the Move gizmo (W) does the same in the view.
Joint rotation How far this joint was turned on itself at rest, in degrees around the character's axes. Its axes change, the skin does not move: a limb that bent sideways then bends in the right plane. The Turn gizmo (E) does the same in the view.
Layer name The layer's name, also used by scripts to switch it on or off (game.pose_layer).
Layer on Off, the layer is kept but no longer plays: handy to compare before and after.
Mirror On, the bone on the other side follows, as in a mirror: turning the left arm turns the right one too.
Moment of the move The moment of the move that is shown. Play it, or stop it where the adjustment shows best.
Move the joint alone Off, the joints below the one you correct follow it (moving the shoulder takes the arm and hand along). On, they stay where they were: only this joint moves, and the bone linking it to the next one gets longer or shorter.
Paste the pose Puts the copied angles in this layer. Bones this model does not have are ignored.
Remove this joint Removes this joint from the skeleton: the skin it moved now follows its parent, and the joints it carried are attached to its parent, at the same place. Its animations and adjustments are ignored. The root of the skeleton cannot be removed. Restore it with « Restore the joint ».
Reset everything Removes every adjustment of the layer. The layer stays, empty.
Reset the bone Removes this bone's adjustment: it goes back to the original move.
Reset this joint This joint goes back to the place and orientation the model file gives it. The other corrections stay.
Show the skeleton Draws the bones in the view: one point per bone, clickable. The chosen bone is highlighted, adjusted bones are green.
Skeleton bones All the model's bones, as a tree, with a search. In brackets, what the bone is when recognised (Left hand, Back...); a dot marks an adjusted bone.
Skeleton pose For an animated model: adjust its pose, bone by bone, on top of its moves. The adjustments apply to every character using this model, wherever the move plays in the game.
Take the skin around The new joint takes from its parent the skin that lies beyond it, with a smooth transition around it: it then bends that part, like the tip of a tail or an ear. Off, it moves nothing: it is an attach point.

Creating a move¶

Explained in detail: Creating a move.

Control What it does
Attach to a move This adjustment targets a move the model no longer has (a changed animation file, a deleted move): choose the one it should adjust from now on, or all of them.
Attach to this model A move or pose adjustments whose model is gone (deleted or moved outside the editor) no longer play: attaching them to this model makes them play again. Ctrl+Z undoes it.
Auto keyframe On, turning a bone or moving its point (with the gizmo or its fields) sets a keyframe at the playhead. Off, only a keyframe already set at that moment changes.
Bone offset For the hips (or the root): how much it rises, lowers or moves forward, in the model's units. In a move, a keyframe is set at the playhead; in an adjustment layer, the offset is added to the move. The Move gizmo (W) does the same with the mouse.
Close the loop Copies the first pose to the end of the move: the loop restarts without a jolt. When looping, a bone with a keyframe at the start already goes back to it at the end.
Copy keyframes Keeps the chosen keyframes and their spacing (Ctrl+C, with the mouse over the timeline), to paste them elsewhere in this move or in another one.
Create a move Makes a whole move for this model (a walk, a wave, an attack), keyframe by keyframe, in the timeline. It starts from the rest pose, an existing move or the adjustment layer, and then plays like the moves of the files.
Delete the move Removes this move from the project (Ctrl+Z brings it back). Systems that played it act without it.
Feet on the ground On, turning or moving the hips, or leaning the back, leaves the feet where they are: the legs bend by themselves (to crouch, to lean).
Finish the move Closes the move's timeline and goes back to pose adjustment. The move stays in the project, saved with it.
Interpolation How each bone goes from the chosen keyframes to the next ones: linear (steady), smooth (slow at start and end), stepped (it jumps from one pose to the next).
Move length The length of one round of the move, in seconds. It cannot cut keyframes: move them first.
Move name The name under which systems, timelines and scripts play it (game.play). The same name as a move of the file replaces it everywhere. Renaming it makes the pose adjustments that target it follow, in a single Ctrl+Z.
Moves made in the tool The moves created for this model. Choose one to edit it: the timeline shows its bones and their keyframes.
Onion skin Shows the skeleton a little before (in green) and a little after (in the accent colour) the playhead, in pale lines, to tune a smooth gesture.
Paste keyframes Pastes the copied keyframes at the playhead (Ctrl+V), with their spacing: the first lands at that moment. Bones this model does not have, and whatever would go past the length, are left out.
Paste keyframes here Pastes the copied keyframes at the moment clicked in the timeline, with their spacing: the first lands at that moment. Bones this model does not have, and whatever would go past the length, are left out.
Remove keyframes Removes the keyframes chosen in the timeline (Delete, with the mouse over the timeline). A bone left without keyframes leaves the move. Ctrl+Z brings them back.
Show all Shows the whole length of the move in the timeline. Ctrl+wheel zooms around the mouse, Shift+wheel (or the bar under the tracks) scrolls.
Start from What the move contains at first: nothing (the rest pose), an existing move read as keyframes (to correct it), or the chosen adjustment layer.
Starting move The move copied as keyframes, ten per second, to correct it without touching the original file.

Editor preferences¶

Explained in detail: Preferences.

Control What it does
Accent color The color of the editor's main buttons, of the selection and of its highlights.
Arrow keys step How far the arrow keys nudge the selection (0.1 m by default): Shift for ten times more, Ctrl for ten times less. Page Up and Page Down move it up and down. Each press is undone with one Ctrl+Z.
Automatic backup How often, in seconds, the editor keeps a copy of your work (60 by default); 0 turns it off. The copy is kept separately: it doesn't touch the scene file.
Close preferences Goes back to the properties of what is selected.
Dark theme A dark interface; unchecked, a light one.
Display How the work views are drawn.
Gizmo size The size of the handles that move, rotate and resize the selected object in the view.
Grid step The spacing between grid lines, in meters, and the step for moves when snapping is on.
Interface size Makes the editor's text and buttons bigger or smaller, from × 0.6 to × 3. The change applies when you release the field.
Lines kept How many messages the console keeps; beyond that, the oldest are removed.
Navigation How the 3D view moves with the mouse.
Orbit speed How fast the view turns when you drag it with the right mouse button, around the point under the mouse (or the selection, with Ctrl).
Pan speed How fast the view moves when you drag it with the middle mouse button (the wheel). At the default value, what is under the mouse follows it, near or far.
Place on ground when adding An object added to the scene settles on whatever is below it, instead of staying in the air.
Placement How objects are placed and moved in the 3D view.
Rotation step The step for rotations with the gizmo when snapping is on, in degrees.
Saving The automatic backup of your work.
Scale step The step for size changes with the gizmo when snapping is on.
View anti-aliasing Smooths the objects' edges in the work views, when their upscaling is off. None, the starting setting, keeps the picture as it is. TAA: sharp when the camera moves, the view is redrawn for a few frames after each gesture. SMAA: sharp, with no trail. FXAA: the lightest. Automatic follows the views' quality. The game has its own setting, in Game window.
View indirect lighting Indirect lighting: light bounced by surfaces (a sunlit floor lights the walls, a room is lit through its window), ray traced with a light cache. High quality with ray tracing only; costs about 1 to 2 ms. On by default; off, the views keep the sky's ambient light. The player chooses their own in the game's menu.
View quality How detailed the work views are: shadows, lights, water, glow, distant terrain and plants. Medium, the starting setting, keeps the editor smooth on a large scene, with four views; High shows the picture as the game draws it on a graphics card; Low helps a small computer. The game started with Play keeps its own quality, and the player chooses theirs in the game's menu. A view is only redrawn when something in it changes: at rest, the editor barely uses the graphics card.
View ray tracing The work views' shadows and reflections drawn with real rays of light, on a graphics card that has it (NVIDIA RTX, AMD Radeon RX 6000 and later, Intel Arc). Automatic (the default): only on a dedicated graphics card, in Medium or High quality. On: always, even where the picture may slow down (an integrated chip, Low quality). Off: the usual shadows and reflections. The views only use it if the game allows it (Game window); the player chooses their own in the game's menu.
View upscaling Draws the scene smaller in the work views, then enlarges it by blending the frames that follow one another: a modest graphics card draws faster, and the objects' edges no longer look like stairs. "Anti-aliasing only" keeps the full size and only smooths the edges. The game has its own setting: click "This game", at the top of the scene list, then Game window.
View upscaling method What enlarges the work views' picture when their upscaling is on. None: WorldMint's own. FSR (all cards): AMD FSR, sharper, on every graphics card. DLSS (NVIDIA): on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX card, from the next start of the editor; as for the game, you first read what NVIDIA's license asks and give the folder of its files.
Zoom step How much one notch of the wheel zooms: at 0.9, the view gets 10% closer. The lower the value, the faster the zoom.

Game settings (nothing selected)¶

Explained in detail: The properties.

Control What it does
Activity radius In a large world, in the game: beyond this distance around the player, in meters (in squares of 256 m), objects fall asleep. They still exist, but no longer move, walk or detect anything, until the player comes back. The same on every computer: the game plays the same everywhere. 300 m by default.
Adaptation time The time the eye takes to get used to the dark, in seconds: 1.5 by default. It gets used to light three times faster, like a real eye. 0: at once.
Add a character size Adds a size, with its own walkable areas: twice the typical character at first, to adjust afterwards. Each size is computed separately: add one only for characters really smaller or larger.
Allow ray tracing Ray tracing draws shadows and reflections by casting real rays of light through the scene: more accurate shadows, sharp at the foot of objects and softer further away, and water, metal or glass reflecting the scenery, even what is off screen. Only some graphics cards have it (NVIDIA RTX, AMD Radeon RX 6000 and later, Intel Arc). Allowed (the default), the player chooses in the game's options: Automatic, On or Off; Automatic only uses it on a dedicated graphics card, in Medium or High quality. Turned off here, the game is always drawn without it, with the usual shadows and reflections.
Always active, advised In a large world: how many objects may stay awake everywhere ("Always active") before the editor shows it in red and the agent is warned. Each costs at every moment of the game, wherever it is. Advice, not a limit: the game keeps them all awake. 100 by default.
Anti-aliasing Smooths the objects' edges when upscaling is off (upscaling already does it). Automatic (the default) follows the player's quality: TAA in High and Medium (SMAA in a project created before November 2026), FXAA in Low. TAA: the sharpest when the camera moves, but each picture is rebuilt from the previous ones. SMAA: sharp, with no trail behind what moves. FXAA: the lightest, a little softer. None: stair-stepped edges. The player can change it in the game's menu, Display tab.
Anticipation How far ahead in time a character sees an encounter coming, in seconds. Longer: it steps aside earlier and more calmly. Shorter: it brushes past the others.
Automatic exposure The image slowly brightens when you walk into a dark place (a house, a cave) and comes back when you walk out, without dazzling: like the eye getting used to it. In sunlight, the image does not change. On by default. In the editor, the views show the image once the eye is used to it.
Avoidance quality How many directions each character tries at each step before choosing. High: closer side steps, for more computing. Low: for crowds of several hundred.
Background The color behind everything else, where there is nothing. In the distance, objects fade into it, as if in a haze.
Brighten up to How many times brighter the image can get in the dark: 1 never, 4 by default (a dark room becomes readable without turning into daylight), 16 and more, you see almost as in daylight in a cellar. The night of a day and night cycle is not brightened: only what is darker than the current daylight.
Character height A character's height, in meters: it walks under anything higher (a bridge, a branch), not under anything lower.
Character radius Half a character's width, in meters: it keeps this distance from walls, and doesn't go into a passage narrower than twice that.
Character walking The typical character for whom the walkable areas are computed: its size, and the slope and step it can climb. Enemies and the other characters that move on their own (game.walk_to) follow these areas, and avoid each other on the way. Other sizes (a small animal, a big monster) each have their own areas. Once the scene is saved, these areas are computed and stored in the project: the game reads them back when it starts. To see them: View menu, Walkable areas.
Characters avoid each other Characters that walk on their own (game.walk_to) see each other coming, along with the player and the other characters, and step aside in time instead of bumping into each other or getting stuck in a corridor.
Choose the folder Chooses the folder of the NVIDIA files; WorldMint checks they are all there and signed by NVIDIA.
Darken up to How many times darker the image can get under a very strong light: 1 never (default: a sunny scene stays as you set it), 2 to 4 for a glare when walking out of a cave.
Distant detail What gets simplified in the distance so that large maps stay smooth: lighter versions of models, the objects of a district merged into one, and distant plants as pictures. It all happens by itself, for any model; these settings turn it off or adjust it.
Distant plants as pictures Beyond their drawing distance, trees and bushes become pictures of themselves, taken from 64 angles and always facing the camera: a forest stays visible from afar for almost nothing. The change fades in.
Download from NVIDIA Opens NVIDIA's page to download Streamline and DLSS.
Edit the terrain Selects the scene's Terrain object, to edit its relief, its regions and its sea.
Exposure How much light reaches the image, as on a camera: 1 leaves the light as it is, higher brightens the image, lower darkens it (0.05 to 8). Very strong lights never turn abruptly white. With automatic exposure, it adds to what the eye chose: higher makes the whole game brighter.
Fog altitude The altitude, in meters, where the fog has the set thickness. With thinning, it is thicker below and lighter above.
Fog color The color of what is in the fog, and of the sky behind it: light grey for a mist, bluish for the distance, dark for a night.
Fog distance A fog over the whole scene: at this distance, in meters, it hides almost everything in its color. 0: none, only the soft horizon fade remains. A light mist: 300 to 800 m; a thick fog: 40 to 100 m.
Folder of the NVIDIA files The folder where you put sl.interposer.dll, sl.common.dll, sl.dlss.dll and nvngx_dlss.dll: each must be there, signed by NVIDIA.
Fullscreen The game fills the whole screen, instead of a window.
Game window The window the game runs in: its title, its size, fullscreen.
Glow of bright lights The soft glow around what shines very brightly: a material giving off light, the sun on water or metal, a lamp close by. 0: none; 1 by default, discreet.
Height of this size The height of the characters of this size, in meters: they walk under anything higher, and wade into water up to half this height.
Highest step What a character steps over without a detour, in meters: a stair, a curb. Higher, it's an obstacle to walk around.
Large world For a map of several kilometers: the scene is saved in pieces of 256 m, in a folder next to its file (".tuiles"), and only the pieces that changed are rewritten. In the game, what is far from the player falls asleep (see "Activity radius"). The previous file is kept next to it; unticking puts the scene back in a single file.
Light from the ground The color of the light bounced back by the ground, which lights the underside of objects.
Loading around the player In a large world, in the game: only the scenery near the player (and the camera showing the game) is loaded, in squares of 256 m. The rest is read in the background as you get closer, and freed as you move away: the game starts faster and takes less memory. What carries a tag or a system stays loaded everywhere. A script that looks for a scenery object by its name only finds it while it is loaded: give it a tag. On by default.
Loading budget In a large world, in the game: the most time one frame gives, in milliseconds, to send the models of incoming scenery to the graphics card. Lower means fewer hitches; higher, distant scenery appears sooner. 4 ms by default.
Loading margin In a large world, in the game: loaded scenery is only freed this much farther than the loading radius, in meters, so that a player going back and forth over a limit does not reload it over and over. 128 m by default.
Loading radius In a large world, in the game: the scenery is loaded within this distance of the player, in meters. Never less than the activity radius plus 64 m. Beyond it, the scenery is drawn as simpler merged districts: a larger radius shows more detail, but takes more memory. The same on every computer. 600 m by default.
Max catch-up After a slowdown, the most calculation steps the game catches up on in a single frame (1 to 100).
Max physics bodies How many objects the game's physics can handle at once. It applies the next time the game starts.
Merged districts distance From this distance to the camera, in meters, a 256 m square is drawn merged, fading over a short band. 600 m by default, in High quality: three quarters in Medium, half in Low (an integrated graphics chip).
Merged districts in the distance In the distance, the fixed objects of a 256 m square (a block of houses, a farm) are drawn as a single simplified object, in the average color of each: this is what lets you see a city's skyline several kilometers away. Done in the background, and redone a few seconds after something changes there. The real objects come back as you get closer.
Multiplayer Playing with several players: one player hosts the game on their PC, the others join it by its address, and each has their own character. The game is played on the host's computer: the others see and hear what it sends them. Off by default: a single-player game doesn't change.
Nearest visible distance Anything closer to the camera than this distance, in meters, isn't drawn.
Network port The "door" number through which the other players join the game, on the PC hosting it (48600 by default). Change it only if another program already uses it. On the first start, Windows may ask to allow the game on the network.
NVIDIA DLSS What NVIDIA's license asks before using DLSS, and the folder of its files.
Objects always active In a large world: the objects carrying this system stay awake even far from the player (a messenger crossing the map). Keep it for few objects: what stays awake far away costs at every moment of the game.
Objets toujours actifs dans la scène How many objects of this scene stay awake everywhere: those with "Always active", and those carrying a system ticked "Objects always active". In red beyond the advised number.
Plant pictures distance How far plants are drawn as pictures, in meters from the camera. 1,500 m by default, in High quality: two thirds in Medium, a third in Low (an integrated graphics chip). Beyond, nothing; they don't go further than the detailed terrain around the camera.
Play with several players Starts the game twice on this PC: one window hosts the game, the other joins it as a guest. You try the game from both sides this way, without exporting or typing an address. Stopping the game closes both.
Players at most How many players in a game, the host included: 2 to 8. Up to 4 works anywhere; beyond that, the host needs a good connection. One more player is refused with a clear message.
Radius of this size Half the width of the characters of this size, in meters. A passage narrower than twice that isn't part of their walkable areas: a big monster doesn't go where a small one does.
Rate How many steps the game computes each second: movement, physics. 60 by default.
Rays in the air A slightly hazy air that shows the sun's rays even without fog: 0 none; 0.2 by default, mostly when looking toward the sun; 1 very strong, from the side too. In a room, add a little fog (dust) to see the rays from the windows.
Read NVIDIA's license Opens the DLSS license on NVIDIA's site.
Scattering toward the sun How much brighter the fog and the air shine when looking toward the sun than with your back to it: 0 the same in every direction, 0.3 by default, 0.6 to 0.9 a strong glow around the sun and very bright rays against the light.
Several players Ticked, the game can be hosted and joined. Each guest arrives with a copy of the player's character, near the starting point; scripts see them arrive ("joueur.arrivee") and leave ("joueur.depart"). The catalog's systems don't all handle several players yet: they take care of the host's player.
Shadow distance How far, in meters around the camera, shadows are drawn. Beyond that, they fade out gently. Further away also makes them a little less sharp up close (5 to 2000 m, 150 by default).
Shadow quality How fine the shadows are, from the sun and from lights (fires included). Depends on the computer: low on an integrated graphics chip (a laptop), high on a graphics card; the player can also choose it in the game menu. High: sharp all the way. Medium: a little less far, fewer lights with shadows. Low: blurrier up close, lighter to draw. Off: no shadows at all, the lightest.
Shadow softness Softens the edge of the sun's shadows, like a real sun: sharp at the foot of an object, softer and softer further from it. It is the size of the sun in the sky, in degrees. 0: sharp; 0.5: the real sun; 2 to 4: a hazy sky; up to 10: very soft. A little costlier on a small computer.
Simplified versions of models From afar, each model is drawn with fewer triangles: half, a quarter, then a tenth, computed once on first load and kept in the project. The version is chosen from the object's size on screen, and changes from one to the next with a fade, without a jump.
Size name The name of another character size, the one you read (Small, Large). Each character takes the smallest size it fits in, from the radius and height its script gives it (game.character).
Sky The color of the light coming from the sky: it lights the top of objects, even where the sun doesn't reach.
Sky and light The scene's lighting: the sun's light, the light coming from the sky and the ground, and the background color.
Steepest slope The steepest slope a character climbs, in degrees. Beyond it, the ground isn't a walkable area.
Step of this size What the characters of this size step over without a detour, in meters. A big one often steps over more than a small one.
Sun color The color of the sunlight.
Sun direction Where the sunlight comes from, -1 to 1 on X, Y and Z: with Y positive, the sun is up high and lights the top of objects.
Sun intensity The strength of the sunlight, from 0 (off) to 20.
Sun shadows Objects, terrain and plants cast their shadow in the sun, on the ground, the water and each other. Untick for lighter rendering, without shadows.
Synced with the screen The game's frames follow the screen's refresh rate: the image doesn't tear when it moves.
Technical settings What you almost never touch: how far you can see, the game's pace, the physics limits and the expected size of models.
Terrain The scene's ground: its relief, its regions and its sea. Without a terrain, generate one here; with a terrain, its settings are on the Terrain object.
Thins out with height Above its altitude, the fog halves every so many meters: a valley fog, a mist over water or a swamp (5 to 30 m). 0: the same everywhere.
Title The title of the game window, and the name of the exported game.
Tolerated on-screen gap How far a simplified version may stray from the real model, in screen pixels, before a finer version takes over. 1 by default: the difference doesn't show. Larger, the game is lighter, and distant objects a little less faithful.
Turn DLSS on Turns DLSS on, once the box is checked and a valid folder chosen.
Updates per second How many times per second the host sends each player where the players are and what moves: 20 by default. More is a little more precise and needs more connection; fewer, the opposite. The other players are shown a tenth of a second in the past, so they glide smoothly.

Quality, Balanced, Performance and Ultra performance go from the sharpest to the fastest; "Anti-aliasing only" keeps the full size and only smooths the edges. Works on every graphics card. Off by default.
Upscaling method What enlarges the game's picture when upscaling is on. None: WorldMint's own. FSR (all cards): AMD FSR, sharper, on every graphics card, already in WorldMint; with "Anti-aliasing only" it smooths edges without shrinking the picture. DLSS (NVIDIA): NVIDIA DLSS, on GeForce RTX cards; WorldMint doesn't provide it: choosing it shows you what NVIDIA's license asks (its logo when the game starts and in its credits, approved by NVIDIA), then asks for the folder of its files. Where the chosen method doesn't work, WorldMint's replaces it. The player can change it in the Menu (Display).

Volumetric fog Sunlight shows in the air and in the fog, with the shadow of whatever hides it: rays of light between trees and through windows, a darker mist in the shade, a brighter one when looking toward the sun. The fog keeps its distance and color. Not on a small computer (Low quality): the usual fog. Off by default.

Warning: tiny model A model smaller than this size, in meters, is flagged when it loads as having an unusual size.

The player's animations¶

Explained in detail: The properties.

Control What it does
Add a sequence Creates a switch from one situation to another, which happens when its conditions are true. At first, it waits for the move to finish.
Add a situation Creates a new situation, with a name to change and a move to choose. Sequences then say when the character enters it.
Advanced animation settings Fine-tuning: for each situation, its playback speed, whether it loops, and which one comes first; then the sequences, which say when to go from one to another.
Animation borrowing On, an animation file made for another character is adapted to this one: to its proportions (taller, stockier), even when it comes from another tool whose bones have other names (Mixamo, Unreal, Blender, Unity…). Bones are found on their own, by their name and then by the shape of the skeleton. Off, moves are read as they are, by bone names: useful if the borrowing goes wrong.
Animation files The library files the character's moves come from. An added file is copied into the project; the cross removes it.
Animations The move the character plays in each situation: idle, walking, jumping… The eye shows it on the character, in the scene.
Bone matching For a file made for a skeleton whose bones have other names: the bone of the file each bone of the character follows, found by name or by shape. A bone marked “to check” rests on a weak clue; choose another bone of the file if it is wrong, or “(none)” so that it keeps its rest pose.
By direction The moves used when the character faces the camera instead of turning toward where it goes: backwards, left and right, and the diagonals (forward-left, back-right…) when the file has them. They are blended with the situation's move (forward) according to the direction taken, on 8 directions; a diagonal with no move is shared by its two neighbours (4 directions). Without them, that move is used in every direction.
Condition Adds a condition to this sequence: a value of the character (its speed, whether it's on the ground…), a sign and a number. All its conditions must be true for it to happen.
Dust on landing A little dust rises under the player when they land from a jump or a fall. Untick it for a game where it does not fit (in space, under water).
Feet on the ground On a slope, a step or a terrain, each foot rests on the real ground (moving objects included: a platform, a boat, a vehicle) instead of floating or sinking, and the hips lower as needed, for the foot touching the ground only: the leg the animation lifts follows the body. The engine looks for the legs in the skeleton (a foot, a knee, a hip): without them, nothing changes.
File shown The animation file whose bone matching is shown, when several come from another skeleton.
Largest feet gap The largest height gap corrected under a foot, in meters: beyond it (a ledge, a hole), the foot follows the animation.
Move backwards The move when it walks backwards. Without it, if there is a sideways move, the forward move plays in reverse.
Move to the left The move when it steps sideways, to its left. Without it, the forward move.
Move to the right The move when it steps sideways, to its right. Without it, the forward move.
Preview move The move played in the preview: on the left as the file gives it, on the right replayed on the character. Both should strike the same pose.
Show all bones Also shows the bones with no role in the body: fingers, helper bones. By default, only the body bones (hips, spine, head, arms, legs) and those chosen by hand are listed.

The terrain¶

Explained in detail: The terrain.

Control What it does
Black altitude The altitude, in meters, of the image's black points: the lowest possible.
Blend between regions The width, in meters, of the color blend from one region to the next. 0: sharp borders.
Choppiness The wind on the water, from 0 to 1: 0, round and regular waves; 1, sharp crests, crossing waves, stronger ripples and foam on the crests.
Clarity How far one sees through the water, in meters: small, murky water; large, a clear lagoon where the bottom shows far from the shores.
Color near the shores The color of shallow water, where the bottom can still be seen: turquoise over sand, green in a pond.
Color steps Stylized water: the number of shades from the shore to the open water, from the color near the shores to the deep water color. 0 or 1: a smooth gradient; 3: three crisp color bands.
Deep water color The color of the water where the bottom can no longer be seen: it is the color of the water object.
Facet size Stylized water: the side, in meters, of the flat triangles that make the surface. Large, very low poly water; small, more detail. Waves shorter than three facets do not show.
Facet strength Stylized water, from 0 to 1: at 1, each facet has its own crisp shade, from its slope facing the sun; toward 0, the surface smooths out, with the same waves. Floating things follow the facets in any case.
Foam The white foam, from 0 to 1: on wave crests, along the shores and around whatever touches the water. 0: none.
Foam width Stylized water: the width, in meters, of the white foam band along the shores and around whatever touches the water. In High quality, a second thinner band comes and goes beyond the first. The foam is also set with Foam (0: none).
Import Lays the heightmap as the terrain's relief, with the regions and sea set for “Generate a world”. Ctrl+Z to go back.
Import a relief Makes the map from a heightmap: a grayscale image where white is the highest (preferably a 16-bit PNG, or a raw .raw or .r16 file), like World Machine, Gaea or real-data converters make. You then choose its scale and altitudes. Up to 4 km per side.
Kind of water A ready-made kind of water: sea, stormy sea, lagoon, lake, pond, low poly sea or low poly lake. Choosing one sets the swell, the colors, the clarity and the foam at once; the two low poly kinds also switch the water to the Stylized style. Everything stays adjustable afterwards.
Level The height of the water surface, in meters. Higher, the water covers more land: the sea rises, a lake overflows its hollow.
Meters per point The distance between two points of the image, in meters: a 1,025-point image at 1 m makes a 1,024 m map. A map is at most 4 km per side.
New region A region of your own, to paint wherever you want: name it and give it its color and systems.
Paint this region Takes the Paint tool with this region: click and drag on the ground, in the view, to spread it.
Regions The large areas of the ground: snow, rock, forest, grassland, desert, swamp, beach. Each one is an object with its color, plants and systems. When generating, untick those you do not want: their areas take the most fitting neighboring region.
Remove the terrain Deletes the terrain from the scene, with everything stored in it: its regions, its water, its plants. Ctrl+Z brings it back.
Sea A sea at the coast level: it covers everything lower. It is always there around continents or islands.
Seamless edges The left edge continues the right edge, and the top continues the bottom: a map that can wrap around the world. The game does not cross from one edge to the other yet.
Shape The shape of the land: up to the edge of the map, one continent, several continents or an archipelago. Except with land up to the edge, a sea surrounds it up to the horizon.
Size For the map: its side (256 m, 512 m, 1,024 m, 2 km or 4 km), used at the next generation. Beyond 1,024 m, a large map: the ground is drawn in detail around the camera and simplified in the distance. For a plant: its size compared with its model (× 1: as is, × 2: twice as big).
Style The look of the water, chosen for the game. Realistic: fine waves, reflections of the sky and the scenery, see-through water where the bottom shows. Stylized: for a low poly game, a surface of flat facets, gentle readable waves, bold color steps and crisp foam bands; it costs less. Floating and swimming things follow the drawn waves in both cases. The render quality (Low, Medium, High) only sets the cost: stylized water stays stylized on a powerful computer.
Swell direction Where the waves go, in degrees, like a compass laid on the terrain. The smaller waves stray from it a little.
Variant The number that sets how the world is drawn: the same number, with the same settings, gives the same map again. The button next to it picks another one at random.
Vegetation The plant kinds growing on this region, with their count. Drag a model from the explorer into the frame (a tree, a bush, grass, a rock): it grows over the whole region.
Wakes The wakes and ripples around whatever moves in the water, from 0 to 1: the V opening behind a boat, the churned water in its wake, the rings around a swimmer or a floating crate. They are only drawn: floating things do not feel them. 0: none.
Wave length The distance from one crest to the next, for the biggest waves, in meters. Short: a chop; long: an ocean swell. Shorter waves are always added on top.
Waves The height of the biggest waves, in meters. 0: calm water, like a mirror.
White altitude The altitude, in meters, of the image's white points: the highest possible.
